The situation, read straight

What this actually is

Back office is the pitch Nate called the hardest to land. That's the reason to run it, not the reason to skip it: a lane this hard to open is a lane with no crowd in it.

Nate named the two walls on today's call. First, most target companies track back-office productivity with a homegrown system they built themselves, so it's cheap, it's theirs, and they're content with it. Second, the big front-office WFM vendors now throw in a back-office module for free, so "we already get that with Verint" ends the conversation before it starts. Both walls are real. Both have the same crack in them, and Nate handed us the chisel: those systems are either fed by the agent or they report after the fact. Neither one acts while the work is happening.

That crack is exactly where the product lives. Intradiem watches live queues and acts in the moment. So the motion's job is not to argue we're better, which they already assume and don't care about. The job is to make the cost of the gap feel real enough that a meeting becomes the prospect's own idea. Meetings landed now also stack toward the Back Office Optimizer GA window in September, so this lane pays twice.

The message

The wedge, built from what Nate already knows

Three incumbent shapes, one thesis. We never argue "we're better." We name the gap their current setup can't close and let the cost of it do the selling.

Wall one

The homegrown tracker

Their system knows only what the agent tells it. Log in, the clock starts. Log out, it stops. Twenty-four tasks that should take thirty minutes stretch to sixty or ninety, and the tracker faithfully records whatever pace the agent set. KPIs get calibrated with a stopwatch and a clipboard. The wedge: they built a system that measures effort as reported, not work as it happens, and the difference between those two is paid for every single day.

Wall two

The WFM throw-in

Free, and it costs them the interval. Verint and NICE bundle back-office modules with front-office WFM, so the price objection writes itself. But those tools alert after the fact: fifteen minutes, thirty minutes, end of day, sometimes the next day. The wedge: a report about a backlog is not the same as an action taken while the backlog is forming. The free tool tells you what went wrong yesterday. Nothing in it acts today.

The thesis every message serves

The back office runs on yesterday's report. The cost lives in what happens between reports.

Every email, voicemail, and LinkedIn note in this motion is a variation of that one idea, told in the language of the seat reading it. The claims VP hears it as the backlog that regrows every Monday. The workforce owner hears it as utilization they can't prove. The COO hears it as capacity they're already paying for and can't reach. One idea, four dialects. And when the "big brother" worry surfaces, it surfaces in discovery, not on the phone, so the scripts don't defend against it cold; the meeting deck does, by framing everything as capacity and queue health, never surveillance.

How every account gets worked

The 10-day blitz

Exactly the forcing function laid out on today's call: everywhere at once, calculated, impossible to ignore politely. Any reply stops that person's sequence on the spot and routes to reply handling. By the end of a sprint we have a meeting or an answer; if a whole account produces zero contact, either we picked the wrong problem or deliverability is broken, and we'll know which.

Anatomy of a sprint (per contact, from their entry day)

DAY 1
Email 1 + LinkedIn connect the same moment + call → voicemail: "no need to call back, the email has it."
DAY 2
LinkedIn note: "shot you a note on email, maybe LinkedIn's better" + one sentence + worth 15 min? Execs enter today.
DAY 3
Email 2. A genuinely new angle, never a "just bumping this."
DAY 4
Call 2 → shorter voicemail. Add value, get out.
DAY 5
Email 3. Short, direct, "like the subject says." Expect replies by now.
DAY 6
Value drop. One-pager relevant to their problem. "Saw this, thought it'd be useful." No ask.
DAY 7
Call 3. Reference the one-pager if it was opened.
DAY 8
Call 4. Last live-voice attempt of the sprint.
DAY 9
Breathing room. Catch-up day for late entrants and replies in flight.
DAY 10
The never-breakup. Lighthearted: "maybe wrong time. Here's what we do at a high level, and who for. I'll back off for now."

Be everywhere at once. Nobody remembers touch one by touch two; the sequence works because it stacks. Until they're a customer we never actually break up, we just get lighthearted about backing off. Calls are bullets, not scripts. Nate said it himself: give him the talking points and it turns into a conversation. That's the design.

Channels and the guardrails

Multi-channel, deliverability-safe

Blitz sends go manually from Nate's own inbox, so the Clay campaign cap stays untouched. Timing is a weapon: C-suite inboxes are read on the phone, off-hours.

Subjects and send hours vary on purpose

Four to eight similar emails hitting one corporate domain in a day looks like a blast to a spam filter and to an IT team. So no two contacts at an account ever get the same subject line, send hours stagger through the day, and the exec tier enters on day 2 after the floor seats are already in motion. Same story inside the account, never the same email.

The claims line

What we can and cannot say

The hard law of this motion: no Intradiem back-office number exists in approved form

The public-site figures (returns per dollar, payback windows, productivity lifts) are do-not-send until they clear the Value Repository. What ships: Humana, named and on the record (7X ROI five years in, 2.7M automated actions in 2025, roughly 2 hours of capacity per agent per month), always framed as what the contact-center side proved, never as a back-office result, healthcare targets only. Plus the prospect's own public numbers, attributed and dated. When neither exists, the qualitative frame carries the message, and it's strong enough to.

One entity trap to check before sprint one

Books of business change hands. An account that reads as a clean prospect at the parent level can carry an acquired unit with an existing vendor relationship, and the reverse. Every sprint account gets an entity check (parent, subsidiaries, recent acquisitions) alongside the customer-exclusion check, same as the Canadian-parent lesson from the BMO work.
