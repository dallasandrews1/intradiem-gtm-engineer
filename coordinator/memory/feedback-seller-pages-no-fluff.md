Dallas, Sep 20 2026, reviewing Keegan's index: every section on a seller page has to earn its place by giving the rep something to act on. Nothing that states the obvious, nothing unnecessary, no fluff, no "cool but useless tool".

**Why:** the pages are judged by whether an AE uses them, not by how much work they show. Counters (accounts, people mapped, live-checked, one-pagers), slogans, paragraphs describing what each link is, industry labels on accounts the rep owns, and legends duplicated from other pages all read as showcase, and they push the useful part below the first screen.

**How to apply:**
- Lead every seller page with what the rep does next: the next dated move by owner (the rep first), computed in the browser from the plan so it never goes stale, plus the date that resets the account.
- One primary button, the rest as text links named by the question they answer ("Who enters which wave").
- Cut effort metrics and self-description. Keep dates of read and refresh.
- GTM Engineering's own process rules (exclusion checks, live-profile gates) stay in the data and still govern, but do not show on the seller view.
- Do not show a rep their own CRM back to them, least of all unverified titles and emails.
- An interactive tool stays only if it encodes a decision the rep would otherwise get wrong (the what-to-send picker stays because it says send nothing after a not-now); otherwise a plain table wins.
- Never print live system state (campaign paused or running, replies so far) as static text on a seller page; it goes stale within days and reads as wrong. State belongs in the logs and the rundown.
- A table column that repeats its group heading on every row is cut; keep a column only where the value varies.
- Applies to new AE, AM and partner pages from here on. Showcase framing still belongs on leadership-facing work, see [[deliverable-strength-framing]].
