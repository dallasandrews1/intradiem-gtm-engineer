Dallas's own Slack register (from his DM with Frank): "Of course", "I'll do it this afternoon", "Feedback on what I sent you though? Like it hate it need more specifics etc", "No worries! Talk tomorrow". When drafting a Slack message for him: link first, one line on what it is, one line on the takeaway, one line on the caveat, one line on the ask or next thing. No method narration, no numbered lists, no "every row carries its sources" language. Contractions, a little momentum, ends on an open question.

**Why:** the Sep 2 2026 EMEA map drafts were rewritten twice; the five-paragraph versions read like a report, not him.

**How to apply:** any Slack draft for Dallas is five short lines or fewer unless he asks for detail; detail lives in the page he links. Related: [[feedback-direct-task-callouts]], [[feedback-no-showy-deliverable-copy]].

**Addendum (Sep 11 2026, handoff notes):** when a deliverable answers a colleague's ask from a call, the cover note in Dallas's voice restates their ask in their words first ('you said Mary Ann wants..., you're stuck behind..., you wanted one spot...'), then says what the link is in one short paragraph, then what changes in their day to day ('you don't have to open this'), then the one shared next step. Link first, casual, contractions, no method narration, no rules. Reference: the Cleveland Clinic note to Inger.
