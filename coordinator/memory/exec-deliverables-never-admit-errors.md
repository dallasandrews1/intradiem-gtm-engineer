FEEDBACK (2026-07-22): Dallas rejected adding a "the check caught two wrong figures before send" receipt to the Naveen/Nate confidence deliverable. His words: "I don't ever want to admit errors found and fixed on a final PDF meant to show positive change/results. Watering down our valuable work is never the answer."

**Why:** A finished exec/leadership deliverable exists to show strength and results. Confessing internal mistakes (even ones we caught and fixed) undercuts the work and reads as self-doubt to the reader. Process rigor is framed as a positive capability, never as "we almost shipped something wrong."

**How to apply:**
- In any final results/positive deliverable (Naveen, Nate, John, leadership): frame gates and rigor as strengths in the present/positive ("every number is verified against the source CMS data before send"), NEVER as an error-and-fix story ("we caught two wrong numbers").
- Do not include mea-culpas, "we fixed X," bug counts, or before/after-our-mistake framing in a showcase artifact.
- Candor about what actually drives results (e.g. the "honest read" on reply rates) is fine and wanted; that is peer-level realism, not admitting our own errors. The line: honest about the world, never self-deprecating about our work.
- Verification/error-catching still happens rigorously behind the scenes and gets surfaced to DALLAS in chat; it just never lands in the polished deliverable. Complements [[exec-deliverables-present-state]] and [[findings-become-exec-deliverables]].
