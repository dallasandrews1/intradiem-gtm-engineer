Sep 22 2026. Dallas: "think about what's already created and ready to launch before suggesting to redo everything with this scoring model."

The Stars trigger-cohort page opened on Genna's scoring model and laid out six cohorts as the plan. What was already finished, three lemlist campaigns with copy re-cut and verified on Sep 21, 72 qualified leads loaded, and the 13 disqualified leads pulled that morning, sat in the fourth section. Correct on the facts, wrong in the order. A reader hit a six-cohort build plan before learning a wave was sitting ready to send.

**Why:** ordering is an argument. Putting the new framework first says the finished work is waiting on it, which invites the team to hold a ready campaign behind a model that has open questions in it. Dallas's standing rule is to check project folders and existing assets before speaking hypothetically; this is the same rule applied to how a deliverable is sequenced, not just to what it claims.

**How to apply:** open with what exists and what it is waiting on. Then say plainly what the new work is for, which is usually expansion or the next cycle, not a replacement. In the rewrite: "Three campaigns are already built, corrected and loaded. That is the pre-release wave and it does not need any of what follows. The scoring model below is for what comes after it."

**Sharpened the same day.** Dallas: "this thing Genna built out is something for us to be using moving forward, not something stopping what we already have. it's for things like building out signals etc in lemlist or clay." So a new model is named for what it BUILDS, not for what it evaluates. Genna's nine signals are the sourcing machinery for the next Clay signal columns and the next lemlist campaign intake. They are not a lens to re-judge what is already loaded and corrected. The page now says so in the second line: "Sourcing machinery, not a review of what is already running."

The general rule: when a teammate brings a framework, state what it will be used to build and when it starts applying. Never let it read as a standard that existing work now has to pass.
