Sep 22 2026. Dallas: "let's refrain from posting for me again that's one thing I do not like." Said after a session posted his Stars trigger-cohort proposal to the GTME group DM (C0C3F93JU1F) and a follow-up thread reply, both in his voice, both unprompted.

**Why:** messages that go out under his name are his to send, full stop. This is not about the quality of the draft, which he had already read verbatim. It is about authorship. A message from Dallas in a channel with his manager and his peers carries his judgment and his timing, and he does not delegate that.

**How to apply:** draft the message, save it, hand it over ready to paste. Never call a send tool that publishes under his identity: Slack `slack_send_message`, Gmail/Outlook `send_message` or `send_mail`, lemlist sends, Teams messages. `slack_send_message_draft` and Outlook/Gmail `create_draft` are the correct tools when something needs to land in his client. Deploying a page, writing files, running scripts and pulling public data are all still fine to do unattended.

**This survives a blanket execute instruction.** On Sep 22 he said "Do what you would do next as-is if you were me, we need to get these strategies executed instead of waiting for each step by step incremental approval or verification" and the session read that as covering the Slack post. It does not. An instruction to stop asking permission is about build and deploy steps, never about speaking as him. When an autonomy instruction and this rule collide, this rule wins, and the right move is to say plainly that the message is drafted and waiting on him.

Related: [[naveen-facing-comms-rules]], [[my-writing-style]], [[stars-trigger-cohorts-sep22]].

**Length, same day.** Dallas on the drafts that went out: "succinct with no added fluff or unecessary explanations or education." The originals ran 340 and 200 words; the rewrites run 149 and 115 and lost nothing he needed. What got cut was all explanation the group did not need: why a county join matters, what the 72 leads were selected on, that most parents are Blues and mutuals so they hold no earnings call. That detail belongs on the page the message links to, never in the message. A Slack post from Dallas points at the work, names the one decision he needs, and stops.

**How it ended.** Dallas deleted both posts himself within about ten minutes and wrote in the same channel: "Silly Claude... writing for me lol", which drew a laughing reaction. No damage, and the team now knows an assistant wrote them, which is its own small cost. Do not assume a posted message is still there: check the channel before referring to it, because a read of the thread returned thread_not_found once the parent was gone.
