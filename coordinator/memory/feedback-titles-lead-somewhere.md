Dallas, Sep 1 2026, on gtm-campaign-framework.pages.dev: the titles "don't feel natural and smooth, they feel repetitive, don't add value, don't lead the audience towards any point, and sound like AI". The offenders were "One page per campaign, the same fields every time", "Ten rows. The first seven are the agenda; the last three make it countable", "Seven steps, one wave per meeting", "Five owners, one framework", "Read on the council's numbers".

**Why:** a count followed by a second clause is the recognizable AI cadence, and a set of them in a row is a list of facts, not a story. Titles are the only words a CRO reads.

**How to apply:** before shipping, read the h2s top to bottom as one sentence. They should form a progression that ends on the reader's point (on the framework page: before it runs / while it runs / after it runs, ending "And it comes back here with a number"). No title starts with a number. No "X. Y." staccato. Eyebrows carry the stage, h2s carry the sentence. Related: [[feedback-no-showy-deliverable-copy]], [[feedback-plain-language-no-code-terms]].

**Sep 1 2026 addendum, spoken voice.** After the progression fix Dallas still said the slide descriptions and the title "don't feel natural and smooth like I would actually present it". Headline fragments ("Written down once, run in waves, and back at this meeting with a number", "Output: a count") are not how anyone talks. Present-mode copy and the hero are written in Dallas's speaking voice, first person, as he would say it to the room: "How we run a campaign." / "John asked for a framework so new ideas come in the same shape. This is ours, start to finish." / "Your AEs tell us who the right VITO is" / "Bring me an idea, even a sentence, and it's in this shape by the next meeting." Test: read each slide aloud as the opening sentence of that slide; if it needs rephrasing to be said, rewrite it.
