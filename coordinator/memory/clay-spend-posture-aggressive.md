10 Aug 2026, Dallas: *"Don't worry about the balance, we need to be aggressive with our spending to get more results sooner."*

This changes the default posture on every credit decision. Roughly 86% of the proof budget was still unspent at the point this was set (11,648.8 consumed of ~83,836 ever made available), and the build phase is largely behind him while the send phase has not started. Holding budget against a cleaner report is the wrong trade when the first-90-day pipeline number is what he is measured on.

**What this changes**

- Stop framing credit posture as frugality. "13.9% spent, nowhere near the 60% flag" becomes "86% still deployable, and the intent is to spend it into results." Same fact, correct emphasis.
- Stop leading with tiering discipline ("wave-one rows only, not the full roster"). Tiering is still how it runs, but the headline is the **unit price**: 12.8 credits for a full profile with a work email and a direct dial. A known unit price makes scaling spend a decision rather than a gamble, and that is the argument that supports going bigger.
- Sequencing: volume first, instrumentation alongside, not instrumentation as a gate on volume. An instrument with nothing flowing through it measures nothing.
- Waves should be sized to what the budget supports rather than to a token proof slice.

**The one thing that still sequences ahead of volume:** the customer-exclusion verification pass on real rows. That is a customer-exposure control, not a reporting preference, and aggressive spend makes it more important rather than less, because a bigger wave is a bigger blast radius. See [[wfm-adjacency-customer-leak-open]].
