Three rules Dallas set while finalizing the Jul 16 Norton leadership deck, for ALL exec-facing deliverables:

1. **No internal tracking references.** Execs never see edit history, decision dates, or condition-closures ("per the July 8 decision," "Done · Jul 10"). Everything reads as current system state. Exception: the credit ledger keeps dated rows since the ledger itself is the receipts artifact.

2. **No budget, funding, pending-seat, or resource-gap framing.** Nothing that could read as the engine lacking something. Posture: the engine has everything it needs; the only decision on the table is approval to start.

3. **Frame the why as the job to be done, never as a diagnosis of the current motion or team.** No "throughput ceiling," "one person hand-drafting," "waiting on that thread" — even when accurate, it lands on real people (Nathan, marketing) whose managers are in the room. State it as neutral arithmetic instead (e.g., "26 parents × ~5 seats × 5 touches ≈ 600 researched personal touches per pass = a systems problem").

**Why:** the deck is a final presentation, not an internal document tracking edits; any pending-resource mention hands execs a cheap deferral ("come back when you have X") and undercuts the one-ask design.

**How to apply:** before delivering any exec-facing doc, sweep for decision dates, "updated/edited/now says," budget/fund/seat/access requests, Done-with-date framing, and any language that reads as a diagnosis of a specific person/team's current performance; convert to present-state claims or cut it.
