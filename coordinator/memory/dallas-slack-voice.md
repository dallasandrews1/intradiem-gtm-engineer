Jul 31 2026: Dallas rejected two channel kickoff posts I drafted as "too formal, doesn't sound like me." Sampled 20 of his real Slack messages the same day to correct it.

His actual Slack register, internal messages to reps and to Naveen:
- Short. Two to five lines, often one. Multi-paragraph only when laying out specifics like pricing.
- Frequent lowercase starts: "also, go onto lemlist...", "working on getting you and Nate setup...", "sending you the email here in a few".
- Asks, doesn't direct: "Hey can you setup your email here for me?", "connect your linkedin account for me too when you can please".
- Contractions and shorthand: asap, gonna, "I'll have at least one sequence lined up and ready to go by Monday".
- Enthusiasm bursts with caps for emphasis: "we are gonna be on fire in the next 30 days", "outbound pipeline MACHINE".
- Ends with an emoji regularly: :muscle::skin-tone-4:, :sunglasses:, :raised_hands::skin-tone-4:, :innocent:, plus "lol".
- Zero structural scaffolding. No headers, no labeled bullet sections, no "what I need back from you:" framing.

**Why:** my drafts read like an operating doc pasted into Slack. The written plan voice (Naveen deliverables, HTML docs) is a different register from how he actually talks to Nate and Jack, and mixing them makes the message land as corporate.

**How to apply:** for any internal Slack message drafted in his voice, write it the way he'd thumb it out, then cut a third. Save the structured framing for the docs. Related: [[dallas-voice-professional-vs-personal]], [[no-aiisms-house-style]].
