Two rules for anything drafted for Naveen Thilagan (Director, Product Strategy, Dallas's manager):

1. **No explaining.** He knows the products, the ICPs, the competitive landscape and the nuance cold. Cut any sentence that teaches him something, justifies why a thing matters, or defines a term. State what was built, what was decided, what's needed back.
2. **Never withhold ideas he asked for.** When he asks for help on something, every idea about that thing goes in the message. On Aug 6 2026 the instinct to "hold an idea for the 1:1 so he isn't overloaded" was wrong: he had explicitly asked for help crafting the slides, so slide ideas belonged in the reply.

**Why:** Under-delivering on an explicit ask reads worse than volume. Dallas would rather send more substance than protect his manager's inbox.

**How to apply:** Draft it, then strip explanations and add back anything held in reserve. Also avoid inventing shorthand he never used, e.g. citing a slide by its page counter ("13/19") instead of its title. See [[deliverables-folder-convention]].
