# Messaging Guide — the voice and structure of every sequence

This is the heart of the skill. The strategy documents earn the meeting *plan*; these messages earn the meeting. Follow this guide exactly — it was reverse-engineered from sequences that booked meetings on day one of sending.

## 1. Product facts (Intradiem) — the ONLY numbers you may use

Use these verbatim. Never invent, round differently, or "improve" a stat.

- Contact centres lose **30–40 minutes per agent per day** to unmanaged idle time — even well-run, award-winning ones.
- Intradiem recovers **15–20 minutes per agent per day** automatically.
- The payback is **contractually guaranteed** (say "payback contractually guaranteed" or "guaranteed payback").
- It **sits on top of the existing WFM** — never replaces it. Also integrates natively with major CCaaS platforms (Genesys Cloud, NICE, etc.).
- What it does with the time: adherence interventions/nudges, break re-optimisation, **training, coaching and wellbeing delivered into idle time as it appears**, in real time.
- It is **rules-based** — no models to govern, no AI governance burden (use this with AI-forward accounts).
- Scale proof point: 15–20 min/agent/day across a large estate = "**a triple-digit headcount equivalent — without a single reduction**" (four-figure for 5,000+ agent estates).
- "You're paying twice for intraday drift — once for whoever firefights it, once for the agent idling through it."
- Qualification floor: **200+ agents** front office. If unsure of scale, qualify on the first call, never in writing.
- **The ask is always a *benchmark conversation*, never a demo.** Offer "the benchmark of what top-quartile [vertical] operations automate intraday versus what's still manual — you keep the data either way."

## 2. Voice rules

- **Under ~110 words per email.** Email 2 shorter than email 1. Breakup shortest of all.
- Open with THEM (their signal, role, situation) — never with Intradiem. The product enters in sentence 2 or 3 at the earliest.
- One idea per email. Email 1 = the hook + what we do. Email 2 = one new angle or number, never a "just bumping this". Email 3 = graceful breakup that leaves the door open and names the re-entry condition.
- Write like a sharp colleague, not a vendor: contractions, short sentences, occasional fragments. No "I hope this finds you well", no "quick question", no "circling back", no exclamation marks, no jargon like "synergies" or "solutions".
- Sign off with the first name only ("Jack") — no title block in the drafted copy.
- Give every persona a named **worldview sentence** they'd nod at. Planners: "the plan is right at 8am and wrong by 10." Ops directors: "growth becomes contact volume." Performance heads: "the biggest number never makes your dashboards." Complaints owners: "the rushed call becomes the repeat contact becomes the complaint."
- End email 1 and 2 with a low-friction question: "Worth a conversation?" / "Worth a look at how that lands on your queues?" Never propose a specific time in email 1.
- Breakup emails wish them well *specifically* ("Good luck with the close and the new hub — big year"), never generically.

## 3. Sequence architecture

Standard cadence per contact (state it at the top of the doc once):
`Email 1 → LinkedIn connect (day 2) → Email 2 (day 4) → LinkedIn message (day 7) → Email 3 breakup (day 11)`, with **phone calls between every touch for planning/WFM/performance personas** (they answer; ops directors get the emails).

Every full-sequence contact gets exactly five assets, in this order, with these exact bold labels:

```
### [Name] — [Title] (ROLE TAG, e.g. PRIMARY / evaluator, phone-first / new-exec play)

**Email 1 — subject: `[lowercase, intriguing, no caps-lock selling]`**
> [body as blockquote]

**Email 2 — subject: `re: [shortened email-1 subject]` (day 4)**
> [body]

**Email 3 — breakup, subject: `[closing the loop / parking this / off your plate]`**
> [body]

**LinkedIn connect (≤300 chars):**
> [must actually be ≤300 characters — count]

**LinkedIn follow-up (day 7):**
> [short pitch + benchmark offer]
```

Exceptions (see §5): execs get ONE exec email + optional connect note, no sequence. Prior-no contacts get a quarantine block. Do-not-sequence accounts get a LinkedIn-only community touch.

## 4. Persona → angle library

Pick the angle by what the title *owns*. Same numbers everywhere; different framing.

| Persona | Angle | Key lines |
|---|---|---|
| Head of Resource Planning / WFM | The intraday layer WFM never finished. Practical, no dumbing down. | "The plan is right at 8am and wrong by 10 — your team absorbs the drift by hand." "Sits on top of your existing WFM." "Planning manages exceptions, not everything." |
| Ops / Contact Centre Director (estate owner) | Scale maths + timing events. | Triple-digit headcount equivalent, "delivered as productivity while you're hiring, not as redundancy." Tie to their live event (new site, merger, platform migration). |
| Performance / Operational Excellence | The last unmeasured variance. | "What's the biggest number that never makes your dashboards?" "Paying twice for intraday drift." "A business case with a contractually guaranteed number." |
| Customer / Service Director | The one lever moving cost AND service the same direction. | "Coaching happens 'when we're quiet' (never)." Recovered time funds daily coaching/wellbeing without CSAT dipping. |
| Complaints / Quality owner | Upstream lever. | "The rushed call becomes the repeat contact becomes the complaint." Fewer rushed contacts upstream, fewer complaints downstream. |
| Transformation / Automation lead | Highest-certainty item on the roadmap. | "Guaranteed number in a portfolio full of modelled benefits." "Rules-based — nothing new to govern," beats GenAI on certainty. |
| Group / C-level exec | Air cover only (see §5.3). | One-line why-now + "I've written to [named colleagues] with the detail — if it's worth their time, a nudge from you is all I'd ask." |
| New-in-role exec (≤6 months) | First-year visible win (see §5.1). | |

Vertical translation: keep the mechanics identical, swap the pressure. Insurance → claims surges, FCA Consumer Duty, mergers. Utilities → price-cap cost pressure, Ofgem service scores, winter surge. Telco → churn, Ofcom complaints league tables. Banking → FCA, branch-to-contact-centre migration. Healthcare → patient access targets. BPO → client SLAs and margin per seat. Mutuals and public-facing brands: **service up, never heads down** — skip all headcount framing.

## 5. Situational plays

**5.1 New-in-role (≤6 months):** congratulate; name the moment ("the listening tour ends and the agenda gets written"); position as "a visible first-year win that needs no restructure"; email 2 names the internal pressure-testers; breakup respects the brutal first-quarter inbox. Subject pattern: `a first-hundred-days win with a guaranteed number`.

**5.2 Win-back / ex-customer (or contact who used the product elsewhere):** never pretend to be a stranger and never plead. Open "You'll know [product] from your [company] days, so I'll skip the intro and give you the update instead." Pitch = "the product has moved a long way" + a live decision event. Never bad-mouth the competitor they switched to — they may have chosen it. Qualify *why* they switched live on a call, never guess in writing.

**5.3 Exec air cover:** one email, ≤80 words, sent only AFTER an operational thread is live. Must name the colleagues already engaged. "Exec notes referencing named colleagues land 10x better than cold ones."

**5.4 Prior no:** quarantine. Write the rules into the doc: no sequence, no cold re-approach, never mention the rejection to their team. Draft ONE re-approach email that (a) acknowledges their exact words, (b) leads with what's materially changed, (c) asks "if the team thinks it's worth an hour, would you take their word for it?" — and fires only on a named trigger (internal thread live, or a relevant leadership change).

**5.5 Prickly gatekeeper (e.g. resource planning head known to kill deals):** one ego-forward touch positioning them as the expert — ask for their read on the benchmark, never pitch. "You keep the findings, I'll take the critique."

**5.6 Do-not-sequence accounts (build in-house, never buy):** no email cadence. One no-pitch LinkedIn touch offering benchmark participation; community route only.

**5.7 Blocked email gateway (bounces citing "administrative prohibition"/policy):** mark the account EMAIL CHANNEL DEAD in the doc, stop sending immediately (retries reinforce the block), and convert sequences to phone + LinkedIn talk tracks.

## 6. Intel hygiene

- Private intel (contract renewal dates, internal gossip, anything from a private channel) NEVER appears in writing. Use public-safe phrasing: "renewals mid-integration rarely just roll over."
- Referral names go only in messages to people the referrer actually knows.
- Public signals (funding, sites, results, awards, leadership moves) should be cited specifically and datedly — that's what makes email 1 feel researched.

## 7. Coordination notes

Every account section ends with a `**Coordination note:**` paragraph covering: contact order by day; who is estate owner / evaluator / sponsor / budget-holder-in-waiting; the instruction to hit everyone the same week and *say so* ("I've also dropped [name] a note") so nobody feels gone-around; who pulls whom into a call if they reply; consistency warning ("this team compares notes — keep every message identical on the numbers"); and any account-specific rules (quarantines, channel restrictions, names still to find).

**Bad vs good (the difference that matters):**
- Bad: "Hi John, I hope you're well! I wanted to reach out because Intradiem is the leading intraday automation solution helping contact centers unlock efficiency and transform agent productivity. Do you have 15 minutes this week for a quick demo?"
- Why it fails: opens with the vendor, no signal, no persona worldview, invented superlatives, asks for a demo and a time slot cold.
- Good: any example above — opens with their world, cites a dated signal, one idea, real numbers, benchmark ask, no time slot.
