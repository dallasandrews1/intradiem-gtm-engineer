Do not use the Copy Sharpener as the starting point for writing outreach copy. If the First Draft Engine has not been loaded yet, load it first.

## Background

The Copy Sharpener enforces excellence at the word level. Enterprise C-suite executives receive hundreds of emails per week. Flabby copy gets deleted. Every word must earn its place. The skill applies 9 disciplined rules that strip away filler, enforce the Tenbit++ framework, front-load value, and ensure tone matches persona. It then flags every change and explains why.

**Critical context:** This skill is a VERIFICATION layer, not a CONSTRUCTION tool. The league-first-draft-engine skill produces the draft by thinking from the prospect's chair, locking one idea, writing the whole message as a flowing thought, and passing the Prospect Test. THIS skill then checks that draft against the 9 rules and 20-point checklist. If the Sharpener flags a rule violation but the message reads naturally and passes the Prospect Test, the message wins. If the Sharpener flags something that genuinely breaks the spell (forbidden word, wrong sign-off, League mentioned in Days 1-5), fix it without disrupting the flow. The fluidity of the whole message is more important than any single rule passing.

## CTA Philosophy (North Star)

Every piece of outreach copy exists to accomplish one thing: make the prospect reflect on THEIR problem and want to solve it badly enough to talk to someone about it. These principles override every other CTA rule in this skill when they conflict.

- **Prospect is the hero.** Every email makes THEM the central figure, not Dallas, not League, not the solution.
- **Feel, not tell.** Make them FEEL the problem through the words, not be TOLD about it. Never declare their responsibilities to them ("you're the one who has to close that gap"). Instead, describe the situation so vividly they self-select into it. They should read it and think "that's me" without being pointed at.
- **Self-selection pattern.** Describe the situation so the prospect sees themselves in it. WRONG: "You own the gap between infrastructure and experience." RIGHT: "That gap between infrastructure and experience is where digital leaders are spending their time right now." The first points at them. The second lets them step into it.
- **Meeting is their idea.** The email creates the tension; the prospect resolves it by wanting to talk. You don't ask for the meeting. You make the meeting the obvious next step.
- **CTA connects the dots.** If the CTA doesn't make the meeting feel like it solves THEIR problem, the email failed, no matter how good the body is. The prospect should never feel like they're doing Dallas a favor or agreeing to hear a pitch.

## Workflow Steps

1. **Capture Draft Copy**
   - Paste the exact draft email, LinkedIn message, voice script, or call script
   - Note the intended recipient (CFO, VP Digital Health, CTO, etc.)
   - Note the communication channel (cold email, LinkedIn InMail, voice note, etc.)
   - Note the goal (book call, send business case, technical validation, etc.)

2. **Apply Rule 1: So What Chain**
   - Read each sentence. Ask: "So what? Does the reader care?"
   - Delete sentences that don't answer "why should I care?"
   - Examples of low-So-What sentences:
     - "We're excited to connect with you." (So what? They've heard this 100 times)
     - "Our solution is innovative." (So what? Everyone claims innovation)
     - "We help health plans improve engagement." (So what? Vague, no specific pain)
   - Rewrite to be specific:
     - DELETE: "We're excited to connect"
     - KEEP: "I found member feedback on your app that's relevant to your engagement strategy"
     - Why: Specific observation, recipient-centric, answers "so what?"

3. **Apply Rule 2: Tenbit++ Framework Check**
   - Every email/message should follow: Observation → Insight → Value → Next Step
   - **Observation**: What did you see? Specific to them. (e.g., "Your app is 3.2 stars on iOS")
   - **Insight**: What does it mean? (e.g., "Benefit navigation is the top complaint in 1-star reviews")
   - **Value**: Why does it matter to them? (e.g., "That's why members abandon and call customer service")
   - **Next Step**: What's the ask? (e.g., "Worth 15 mins to talk through this?")
   - If any element is missing or weak, strengthen it
   - If email has extra sentences that don't map to Tenbit++, delete them

4. **Apply Rule 3: Length & Front-Loading Limits**
   - **Cold Email 1**: 80-120 words maximum
   - **Cold Email 2+**: 120-150 words maximum
   - **LinkedIn InMail**: 2-3 sentences maximum (50-80 words)
   - **LinkedIn DM**: 3-5 sentences maximum (50-100 words)
   - **Voice Note**: Under 45 seconds (roughly 100-125 words when spoken at normal pace). Every voice note MUST also include a TL;DL (too long; didn't listen) written summary (2-3 sentences) sent alongside the audio.
   - **Voicemail**: Under 25 seconds (roughly 50-60 words)
   - **Cold Call Opening**: Under 45 seconds total (~100 words). Structure: Opening (8-10 sec), Hook (15-20 sec), Bridge (10-15 sec), CTA (5-10 sec)
   - Front-load value: The first 1-2 sentences must contain the most compelling insight. If reader stops reading after first sentence, they should understand why you're reaching out.
   - Count words. Cut ruthlessly. Every word must earn its place.

5. **Apply Rule 4: Brand-Light Execution (Days 1-5)**
   - Days 1-5 of outreach: Do NOT mention League by name anywhere. Not in subject lines, email bodies, LinkedIn notes, voice scripts, or signatures.
   - **Sign-off Days 1-5: "Dallas"** (first name only, warmer and more personal)
   - **Sign-off Day 6+: "Dallas Andrews"** (representing the company once League is named)
   - Never sign as "Dallas Andrews, League" or "Dallas Andrews | League" in any email.
   - Frame solution as category: "member engagement platform," "activation solution," "digital health platform"
   - WRONG: "At League, we help health plans activate members." Also WRONG: "Best, Dallas Andrews, League"
   - RIGHT: "Most plans approach member engagement reactively. Data-driven activation changes that." Sign-off: "Dallas"
   - League can appear starting Day 6, but sparingly and only when it adds credibility
   - Check every sentence AND every signature: Does it unnecessarily mention League?

6. **Apply Rule 5: Forcing Function Specificity**
   - The opening must reference the specific forcing function or pain point
   - VAGUE: "I saw you're in the health insurance space."
   - SPECIFIC: "Your engagement rate dropped 2 points YoY, and member services cost is up 15%."
   - VAGUE: "You probably care about member experience."
   - SPECIFIC: "Your app is at 3.2 stars on iOS. Benefit navigation is the top complaint."
   - If draft doesn't reference a specific, data-backed forcing function, rewrite it

7. **Apply Rule 6: CTA Construction and Escalation**
   - **Connector sentence (REQUIRED before every CTA question):** Add one sentence just before the CTA that connects the prospect's specific role/pain to why THIS meeting matters to THEM specifically. Make them see themselves in that sentence. This is what makes them want to say yes.
   - **Every CTA must specify WHAT the meeting is about.** The prospect needs to know what they're saying yes to. WRONG: "Worth 15 minutes?" RIGHT: "Worth 15 minutes to walk through what the winning rebid narratives actually looked like?" Frame the "what" as something useful to them whether or not they ever buy from League.
   - **One question, one ask.** Never stack two questions back-to-back. It makes the email look longer than it is and dilutes the ask.
   - **Never repeat the "how"** in the CTA if it's already in the paragraph above. The CTA adds the "why meet" not a recap.
   - **Format-specific CTA rules:**
     - Emails (longer format): Specify what the 15 minutes is for. "Worth 15 minutes in the next couple weeks to walk through [specific value]?"
     - LinkedIn DMs (shorter format): "Worth a quick conversation?" is enough. The context is already in the 2-3 sentences above it. Don't make a DM feel like an email.
   - **Seniority-calibrated timing:**
     - Directors and mid-level: "early next week" or "this week" is fine
     - VPs, SVPs, C-suite: "in the next couple weeks." They don't open calendars in 5 days for cold outreach.
   - **Cold outreach calibration**: For first cold emails to VPs/SVPs/C-suite, always use "in the next few weeks" or "in the next couple weeks." Never use "this week or next" for senior cold outreach — it signals desperation and gets deleted.
   - **Escalation schedule:**
     - Email 1: Soft CTA ("Think it's worth finding 15 min to [specific value]?")
     - Email 2: Slightly stronger ("Worth a quick call to walk through [what you found]?")
     - Email 3: Direct ("Let's set up 15 mins. I've got [specific deliverable] ready.")
     - Email 4: Formal ("Available [timeframe]?")
     - Email 5: Final ("This is worth your time. I'm here whenever the timing lines up.")
     - Week 2: Move to phone/voice as channel
   - **Day 1 Email Three-Beat Close (REQUIRED):** First cold emails use a specific three-beat CTA structure:
     1. Short proof point sentence about a peer/comparable plan ("A COO at a comparable plan used the review period to get ahead of exactly this.")
     2. Standalone "Might be helpful/useful/valuable to walk you through..." sentence ("Might be helpful to walk you through how she structured the operational readiness timeline.")
     3. Blank line, then "Worth 15 min in the next few weeks?" as its own separate line
     4. Blank line, then bare "Dallas" sign-off
     Do NOT merge these into one flowing sentence. The three beats create intentional descending rhythm that contrasts with the smooth body prose above. The Rick Hopfer HMSA Day 1 email is the gold standard.
   - **NEVER end on a statement.** "I think 15 minutes would be useful" is a declaration, not a question. Every CTA must be a question the prospect can respond to.
   - **NEVER use commanding CTAs.** "Let me send over a couple times" tells the prospect what to do. Ask, don't command.
   - **NEVER use weak/passive CTAs.** "I can walk through what I found" or "if at all" hedge and undercut confidence.

8. **Apply Rule 7: Persona & Tone Matching**
   - CFO persona: Metrics-driven, concise, ROI-focused. Tone: executive summary, numbers, no jargon.
   - VP Operations persona: Process-driven, efficiency-focused. Tone: operational, specific, FTE impact.
   - VP Digital Health persona: Engagement-driven, innovation-focused. Tone: consultative, partnership, NPS/metrics.
   - CTO persona: Technical-focused, risk-averse. Tone: technical specification, architecture, third-party validation.
   - CMO persona: Brand/positioning-focused. Tone: strategy, differentiation, competitive.
   - Check every sentence: Does it match this persona's values and tone?
   - WRONG for CFO: "We're committed to empowering your team with innovation."
   - RIGHT for CFO: "Implementation typically generates 20-25% member services cost reduction within 12 months."

9. **Apply Rule 8: Linguistic Guardrails**
   - NO FORBIDDEN WORDS: leverage, synergy, cutting-edge, best-in-class, empower, seamless, robust, transform, transformation, catalyst, paradigm, innovative, comprehensive
   - NO vague language: "exciting," "great," "amazing," "powerful"
   - NO qualifying phrases: "I think," "in my opinion," "hopefully," "I believe"
   - NO defensive language: "I know you're busy," "I'm sure you get a lot of emails" (acknowledges but doesn't solve)
   - NO clichés: "circle back," "touch base," "reach out," "at the end of the day"
   - NO em dashes (—) in prospect-facing copy. They are an AI writing giveaway. Replace with periods, semicolons, commas, or colons depending on context. Internal notes and planning sections can keep them.
   - NO engagement tracking references in prospect-facing copy. Never mention email opens, link clicks, or engagement signals. That data is internal planning context only. Referencing it comes across as surveillance and kills trust.
   - NO specific go-live timelines unless verified. Use "weeks, not quarters" instead of "12-16 weeks."
   - NO robotic or fragmented copy. Emails should read like a real person wrote them in one sitting. Add connective tissue between ideas. Vary sentence length. Mix short punches with longer flowing sentences. Specifically: consecutive short declarative sentences in body paragraphs must be merged with commas, 'and', 'but', or 'because' to create natural speaking rhythm. Two or three choppy sentences in a row ('The filing went in. The timeline started. The pressure is real.') should become one flowing sentence ('The filing went in April 9, and the timeline started moving from theoretical to real.'). This body fluidity rule does NOT apply to the Day 1 three-beat CTA close, which intentionally uses short punchy separate elements.
   - NO "Result:" transitions or other formulaic structures.
   - Subject lines must be short, lowercase, conversational. No title case.
   - Read draft for these violations. Find and replace.

10. **Apply Rule 9: Send-Ready Test (20-Point Checklist)**
    - [ ] Does it start with specific observation (not generic greeting)?
    - [ ] Is the forcing function data-backed (not assumed)?
    - [ ] Does it answer "so what" for the reader?
    - [ ] Is it Tenbit++ (Observation → Insight → Value → Next Step)?
    - [ ] Is it under word count limit for this channel?
    - [ ] Does first sentence hook the reader?
    - [ ] Is CTA appropriate to email position in sequence?
    - [ ] Does CTA have a connector sentence linking their role/pain to why the meeting matters?
    - [ ] Does CTA specify WHAT the meeting is about (not just "Worth 15 minutes?")?
    - [ ] Is there only ONE question in the CTA (no stacked questions)?
    - [ ] Is the prospect the hero (never told what their job is, never "you own" or "you're the one")?
    - [ ] Does it make them FEEL the problem (self-selection) rather than being TOLD about it?
    - [ ] Does it match persona tone?
    - [ ] Does it avoid all FORBIDDEN WORDS?
    - [ ] Does it avoid vague language?
    - [ ] Is League absent from Days 1-5 copy (including signatures)?
    - [ ] Is sign-off "Dallas" (Days 1-5) or "Dallas Andrews" (Day 6+)?
    - [ ] Are there zero em dashes in prospect-facing copy?
    - [ ] Does it sound like a peer, not a salesperson?
    - [ ] Would you send this from your own email?
    - [ ] Do body paragraphs flow naturally without staccato chains of short declarative sentences?
    - [ ] Does the Day 1 email CTA follow the three-beat close (proof point → value statement → "Worth 15 min..." separate line)?

11. **Create Changes Log**
    - For every change made, document it:
      - Original text
      - Sharpened text
      - Rule applied
      - Rationale
    - Example:
      - **Original**: "We're excited to partner with you and help you improve member engagement."
      - **Sharpened**: "Your app engagement is down 2 points YoY."
      - **Rule**: So What Chain + Forcing Function Specificity
      - **Rationale**: Original is vague and cliché. Sharpened version is specific and recipient-centric.

12. **Return Sharpened Copy + Changelog**
    - Output: Sharpened version of copy (ready to send)
    - Output: Side-by-side comparison showing original vs. sharpened
    - Output: Changelog with rationale for every edit

## Cold Call, Voice Note, Voicemail, and Vidyard Standards

The Copy Sharpener applies to ALL channels, not just email and LinkedIn. These spoken-word channels require extra attention because bad scripts sound worse when spoken aloud.

### Cold Call Script Standards
- **Maximum 45 seconds spoken** (~100 words). Prospect decides in 8 seconds whether to keep listening.
- **Structure**: Opening (8-10 sec, name + one credible sentence), Hook (15-20 sec, specific data-backed observation about THEIR business), Bridge (10-15 sec, connect to peer result), CTA (5-10 sec, assumptive).
- **Opening MUST NOT**: Start with "I know you're busy." Use company name in the first sentence. Ask "Do you have a minute?" Say "How are you?"
- **Hook MUST**: Reference something specific to this account (app rating, earnings data, member count, initiative, hire, regulatory pressure). Generic hooks get hung up on.
- **CTA MUST be assumptive**: "I think it'd be worth 10 minutes" not "Would you be interested in a call?" or "Want 15 minutes to see the path?"
- **Objection handlers** must be included for: "Who is this?", "Not a good time", "Send me an email", "We're already working on this."
- **Tone test**: Read it aloud. Does it sound like a real person talking? If it sounds like a script, rewrite it. Mixed sentence lengths. Some short. Some longer. Natural rhythm.

### Voice Note Standards
- **Under 45 seconds** (~100-125 words at natural speaking pace).
- **Structure**: Quick intro (first name and last name, no title, no company), one specific credible reference (their earnings call, app data, a recent hire they made), one insight that shows pattern recognition across plans, soft next step.
- **Tone**: Like leaving a message for a colleague you respect. Warm but brief. No pitch language.
- **NEVER use**: "I'd love to," "I was hoping to," "I wanted to reach out," "I think you'd be a great fit." These signal lower status and sales intent.
- **ALWAYS include**: One specific, verifiable reference to something about their business. This is what separates a voice note from spam.
- **TL;DL (Too Long; Didn't Listen)**: Every voice note MUST be accompanied by a short written TL;DL summary (2-3 sentences max) sent alongside it. The TL;DL captures the core insight and CTA in text form so the prospect can engage even if they don't play the audio. Sending both is getting significantly more replies than voice note alone.

### Voicemail Standards
- **Under 25 seconds** (~50-60 words). Most voicemails get deleted at 15 seconds.
- **Structure**: Name, one sentence of context (what you noticed), callback reason (what you have for them). That's it.
- **No pitch. No value prop. No company description.** Just enough to earn a callback.

### Vidyard Script Standards
- **Under 3 minutes**. Structure: Screen share of their actual app reviews or public data (30 sec), walk through 2-3 specific friction points you found (60-90 sec), reference one peer outcome without naming the peer (30 sec), soft CTA (15 sec).
- **Tone**: Like showing a colleague something interesting on your screen. Conversational. "So right now I've got [their app] reviews on screen. Let me scroll through some of these."
- **NEVER**: Turn it into a product demo. Never show League's product. Show THEIR data and THEIR problem.

### LinkedIn Touch Logic (Channel Selection)
When a roadmap includes a LinkedIn touch, the channel depends on connection status. Always include both versions so Dallas can decide in real-time.

- **Connection pending / not accepted:** Send as LinkedIn InMail (text). Include a voice note DM version underneath so Dallas can switch immediately if the connection is accepted before send.
- **Connection JUST accepted (within 24-48 hrs):** Send a SHORT TEXT DM first. Light touch, reference yesterday's email, conversational. Voice note cold to a fresh connection feels too aggressive. Save voice note for Day 4 escalation.
- **Long-standing 1st degree connection:** Send as LinkedIn VOICE NOTE DM. Shorter, conversational, spoken-word pacing.
- **All voice note DMs:** Include a TL;DL text message immediately after the voice note. 2-3 sentences capturing the core insight and CTA. Prospects who can't or won't play audio still get the value.
- **LinkedIn disabled:** Skip LinkedIn entirely, use phone voicemail instead.
- **Email-only contacts (not on LinkedIn):** No LinkedIn touches at all.
- **Voice notes work best as ESCALATION (Day 4+)** after text touches have established familiarity, not as the first DM to a fresh connection.
- **LinkedIn connection requests:** Always send blank. No personalized message. Never script connection request messages in roadmaps.

### Default Timeline: Day 1 Blitz
- All cold emails to all tiers go out on Day 1. Do not stagger first emails across multiple days.
- Follow-ups (InMails, voice notes, deep-dives, foundation emails) cascade from Day 2 forward.
- Pincer movement starts Day 4-5, not Day 7-8.
- Compress to ~7 days, not 10. Time is always of the essence.

### Multi-Persona Differentiation (All Spoken Channels)
When generating scripts for multiple personas within the same account, each script MUST:
- Reference a DIFFERENT forcing function or pain point specific to that persona's role
- Use vocabulary appropriate to that persona (CFO hears "margin," "cost per member," "HBR"; CTO hears "architecture," "API," "integration"; COO hears "scaling," "operational complexity," "vendor consolidation")
- Have a DIFFERENT opening. Never start two scripts in the same playbook the same way.
- Have a DIFFERENT CTA framing. Vary between time-based ("15 minutes this week"), value-based ("I've got the deployment data"), and curiosity-based ("I think you'd find this interesting").

### CTA Standards (All Channels)
All CTAs must follow the CTA Philosophy (North Star) at the top of this skill. The prospect is the hero. The meeting is their idea. Feel, not tell. These escalation examples show tone progression, but every CTA must also include a connector sentence and specify what the meeting covers (see Rule 6).
- **Days 1-2**: Soft. "Given [connector to their world], think it's worth finding 15 min to walk through [specific value]?"
- **Days 3-5**: Moderate. "Worth a quick call to walk through [what you found]?"
- **Days 6-8**: Direct. "I've got [specific deliverable] mapped out. Worth 15 minutes in the next couple weeks?"
- **Days 9-10**: Confident close. "This is worth your time. I'm here whenever the timing lines up."
- **NEVER use**: "Want 15 minutes to see the path?", "Compare notes?", "Pick your brain?", "Touch base?", "Circle back?", "Does that make sense?", "Would you be interested?", "I can walk through what I found", "if at all"
- **ALWAYS**: Make the CTA match the sequence position. Soft early, direct late. Desperate never. One question only, never stack two.

## Canon Constraints

- **Tenbit++ Framework**: All external communications follow Observation → Insight → Value → Next Step
- **Pincer Rule**: Communications to VPs and Directors focus strictly on Operational Relief, never Brand Vision
- **Brand-Light Execution**: Do NOT mention League by name anywhere in Days 1-5 outreach. Not in subject lines, email bodies, LinkedIn notes, voice scripts, or email signatures. Sign as "Dallas" (Days 1-5) or "Dallas Andrews" (Day 6+). Frame solution as category. League can appear starting Day 6, but sparingly.
- **FORBIDDEN WORDS**: Never use leverage, synergy, cutting-edge, best-in-class, empower, seamless, robust, transform, transformation, catalyst, paradigm, innovative, or comprehensive
- **FORBIDDEN CTA PHRASES**: Never use "compare notes," "pick your brain," "touch base," "circle back," "does that make sense," "would you be interested," "want 15 minutes to see the path"
- **Strict Punctuation**: No em dashes in prospect-facing copy (AI writing giveaway). Replace with periods, semicolons, commas, or colons. Internal notes can keep them.
- **Named Outputs**: Every deliverable gets saved as a file. Nothing lives only in chat.
- **MEDDPICC Discipline**: After every call transcript, flag empty or weak MEDDPICC fields.
- **Copy Quality Gate**: Before saving any output, re-read every piece of outreach copy and verify: (1) no forbidden words or canned phrases anywhere in copy, (2) word counts within spec for each format, (3) CTAs match the escalation schedule for the sequence position, (4) no League mentions in Days 1-5 including signatures, (5) no two scripts open the same way, (6) every CTA has a connector sentence and specifies what the meeting covers, (7) prospect is the hero, never told what their job is, (8) no em dashes in prospect-facing copy, (9) sign-off is "Dallas" Days 1-5 and "Dallas Andrews" Day 6+, (10) no stacked questions, (11) every voice note has an accompanying TL;DL text summary, (12) body paragraphs flow with merged sentences and connective tissue (no staccato), (13) Day 1 email CTAs follow three-beat close structure. If anything fails, rewrite inline before saving.
