# CMS Star Ratings Reference

**Steward**: Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS)
**Applies to**: Medicare Advantage (MA) plans and Part D prescription drug plans
**Reporting**: Annual; ratings released each fall for the following plan year
**Scale**: 1–5 stars (0.5 increments); 5 = highest quality

---

## Why Stars Matter

Star ratings drive significant financial and operational consequences:
- **Quality Bonus Payments (QBP)**: Plans rated 4+ stars receive bonus rebates (can be worth hundreds of millions annually)
- **Enrollment**: Higher-rated plans attract more beneficiaries; CMS markets 5-star plans year-round
- **Formulary & benefit flexibility**: 5-star plans have special enrollment period privileges

## Triple-Weight Measures (Highest Priority)

These measures count 3x in the Star calculation — **with a critical 2026 exception noted below.**

| Measure | Abbreviation |
|---------|-------------|
| Medication Adherence for Diabetes Medications | MAD |
| Medication Adherence for Hypertension (RAS Antagonists) | MAH |
| Medication Adherence for Cholesterol (Statins) | MAC |
| Statin Use in Persons with Diabetes | SPD |
| MTM Program Completion Rate for CMR | CMR |

> ⚠️ **2026 Exception**: MAD, MAH, and MAC are **temporarily reduced to single-weight (1x) for the 2026 measurement year** as CMS introduces sociodemographic status (SDS) risk adjustment to these measures. They are expected to return to triple-weight in 2027. CMR is also a **display-only measure in MY 2025 and 2026** (not counted in ratings) while CMS adjusts for a significantly expanded eligible population; scoring resumes in 2027.

> **Clinical design implication**: Even at single-weight, medication adherence remains the highest-leverage domain given its return to triple-weight in 2027. Maintain investment. The SDS adjustment also means plans serving high-risk populations (LIS, dual-eligible) will have more equitable scoring.

## CAHPS in Stars

CAHPS survey measures are a significant component of Stars. They are **not claims-based** — they require member outreach and survey response. Key CAHPS measures in Stars:

- Overall Rating of Health Plan
- Getting Needed Care
- Getting Care Quickly
- How Well Doctors Communicate
- Customer Service
- Rating of Drug Plan (Part D)

> ⚠️ **2026 Weighting Change**: CAHPS and patient experience measures were reduced from **4x to 2x weight** in 2026 (down from ~60% of total Star Rating to ~22%). This is a major structural shift. Clinical outcome measures now carry relatively more weight. However, experts advise plans not to deprioritize member experience — CAHPS performance directly enables members to access care and close clinical gaps.

## EHO4All — Excellent Health Outcomes for All (Health Equity Index)

**NEW in 2026**: CMS introduced the **EHO4All** framework, which rewards plans that demonstrate improved quality for underserved populations (low-income subsidy recipients, dual-eligible members, members with disabilities, rural populations).

- Replaces the prior Reward Factor in the Stars calculation
- Plans that perform well on key measures *for their most vulnerable subgroups* receive a bonus in their overall Star Rating
- Relevant measures include medication adherence, preventive screenings, chronic disease control, and behavioral health follow-up

> **Design implication**: Health equity is now financially scored in Stars — not just a compliance or values exercise. Programs targeting LIS/dual-eligible populations with adherence and care gap interventions have direct Stars ROI.

## HOS (Health Outcomes Survey) in Stars

The Medicare Health Outcomes Survey measures **change in physical and mental health functioning** over 2 years using the VR-12 (a PRO instrument). Two measures:

- **Improving or Maintaining Physical Health (MHP)**
- **Improving or Maintaining Mental Health (MHM)**

> These are the primary PRO-based measures in Stars. Relevant for clinical content targeting functional status, fall prevention, pain management, and behavioral health.

---

## How CMS Calculates Stars

1. **Raw rate** calculated for each measure
2. **Standardized score** (z-score) relative to national distribution
3. **Cut points** set annually using clustering algorithm — cut points shift each year
4. **Measure scores** aggregated into domain scores
5. **Domain scores** combined (weighted) into summary star rating
6. Plans must have sufficient enrollment per measure to be rated (minimum denominator)

> **Implication**: A plan can improve its raw rate and still lose stars if the national average improves faster. Absolute performance is not enough — relative performance matters.

## Timing Considerations

| Activity | Timeline |
|----------|----------|
| Measurement year | Jan 1 – Dec 31 |
| CAHPS survey | Spring of following year |
| HOS baseline | Spring; follow-up 2 years later |
| Ratings published | October (for following plan year) |
| QBP payments | Following plan year |

Plan for content and engagement interventions to have impact within the measurement year window — late-year interventions (Oct–Dec) may not produce claimable events in time.
