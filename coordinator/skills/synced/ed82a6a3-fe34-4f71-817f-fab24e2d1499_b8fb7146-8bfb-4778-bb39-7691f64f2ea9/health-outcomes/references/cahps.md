# CAHPS Reference

**Full name**: Consumer Assessment of Healthcare Providers and Systems
**Steward**: Agency for Healthcare Research and Quality (AHRQ); administered by CMS for Medicare
**Type**: Survey-based patient/member experience measures
**Used by**: Health plans, hospitals, providers, CMS Stars, NCQA HEDIS

---

## What CAHPS Measures

CAHPS captures **member-perceived experience**, not clinical outcomes. It answers: "Did the patient feel they got good care?" This complements claims-based quality measures.

Key dimensions:
- **Access**: Did the patient get the care they needed, when they needed it?
- **Communication**: Did providers listen, explain, and show respect?
- **Coordination**: Did care feel connected and well-managed?
- **Customer service**: Was the plan easy to work with?

## CAHPS Survey Families Relevant to Health Plans

### 1. CAHPS Health Plan Survey (Commercial + Medicaid)
Used in NCQA HEDIS. Core composites:

| Composite | What it asks |
|-----------|-------------|
| Getting Needed Care | Ease of getting referrals, specialist care |
| Getting Care Quickly | Wait times for appointments, urgent care |
| How Well Doctors Communicate | Listening, explaining, respect |
| Customer Service | Plan's helpfulness, information quality |
| Shared Decision Making | Involvement in treatment decisions |

Global ratings (0–10 scale):
- Rating of Health Plan
- Rating of Personal Doctor
- Rating of Specialist Seen Most Often

### 2. CAHPS Medicare Advantage Survey (Part C)
Used in CMS Stars. Composites align with commercial but include:
- Getting Needed Care
- Getting Care Quickly
- Rating of Health Care Quality
- Rating of Health Plan
- Care Coordination
- Health Promotion and Education

### 3. CAHPS Part D (Drug Plan) Survey
- Getting Needed Prescription Drugs
- Rating of Drug Plan
- Customer Service (Part D)

### 4. CAHPS Hospital Survey (HCAHPS)
Used for hospital inpatient experience. Key domains:
- Communication with Nurses
- Communication with Doctors
- Staff Responsiveness
- Communication about Medicines
- Discharge Information
- Care Transition
- Cleanliness & Quietness
- Overall Rating of Hospital
- Recommend Hospital

### 5. Child CAHPS
Used for Medicaid pediatric populations. Captures parent-reported experience of children's care.

## CAHPS Composite Scoring

- Surveys use a frequency scale: Never / Sometimes / Usually / Always
- "Always" or "Yes" responses = positive experience
- **Top-box scoring**: % responding "Always" or most positive option
- **Bottom-box**: % responding "Never" or least positive — used to detect problems

---

## CAHPS in CMS Stars

CAHPS measures contribute approximately **30–35% of the Part C Star rating**. In Stars:
- CAHPS measures are **double-weighted** (2x in summary calculation)
- Results are based on the prior year's survey (18-month lag to Stars publication)
- Plans with < 100 respondents may be suppressed

### Key Stars CAHPS Measures:
| Measure | Domain |
|---------|--------|
| Getting Needed Care | Access |
| Getting Care Quickly | Access |
| How Well Doctors Communicate | Interactions |
| Customer Service | Plan responsiveness |
| Rating of Health Plan (0–10) | Overall experience |
| Care Coordination | Continuity |

## Designing for CAHPS Improvement

When clinical designers or content teams ask how to improve CAHPS scores:

**Getting Needed Care**
- Reduce referral friction; simplify PCP and specialist access
- Provide clear guidance on covered services and how to access them

**Getting Care Quickly**
- Promote telehealth and after-hours options
- Communicate expected wait times and alternatives

**How Well Doctors Communicate**
- Content that helps members prepare for appointments (question lists, shared decision aids)
- Post-visit follow-up materials to reinforce what was discussed

**Rating of Health Plan**
- Reduce member effort to resolve issues
- Proactive outreach around transitions of care and benefit changes

**Care Coordination**
- Attribution of care manager; warm handoffs after ED/inpatient
- Summary-of-care communications

## CAHPS and SDOH

CMS introduced a **Patient Experience Factor (PEF)** to adjust Stars for plans serving high proportions of:
- Low-Income Subsidy (LIS) recipients
- Dual-eligible members (Medicare + Medicaid)
- Members with disability status

Plans serving these populations are adjusted to account for the correlation between social risk factors and lower reported experience. This does **not** excuse lower performance — it contextualizes it.

---

## CAHPS Limitations

- Survey lag: results reflect experience from 12–18 months prior
- Non-response bias: sicker or disengaged members less likely to respond
- Attribution: members may rate experiences with providers the plan doesn't control
- Not linked to clinical outcomes directly — a member can report great experience and still have poor disease control
