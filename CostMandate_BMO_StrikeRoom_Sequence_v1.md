## Account snapshot

### The forcing function

On February 25 2026, BMO took a $202 million pre-tax severance charge and its Chief Financial Officer put a number against it on the earnings call. Rahul Nalgirkar: *"We expect to realize annualized savings of approximately $250 million from the charge, with half realized in 2026 and the remainder in 2027."*

He reaffirmed it three months later. On the Q2 call, May 27 2026: *"We are on track to execute the previously announced efficiency program, which will generate approximately $250 million in annualized savings, half of which is expected to be realized this year."*

That is a forward obligation with a named owner, a public scoreboard and a hard date. The FY2026 half is being delivered. **The FY2027 half has never been publicly sourced.** BMO's fiscal year ends October 31, which means the plan that has to contain it gets built in September and October.

### The tension the whole sequence hangs on

BMO has already conceded the mechanism. At its Investor Day on March 26 2026, Darryl White said BMO is *"automating our own businesses...Freeing capacity allowing teams to focus on higher value work,"* and that BMO expects *"continued savings from AI and digital adoption."*

So the argument is not whether capacity release is the answer. BMO said it is. The open question is where the next increment comes from, given that Aron Levine told the same room that *"More than 80% of our cost takeout target is already under execution"* and that BMO expects headcount to *"remain essentially flat on employee head count over time."*

Eighty percent under execution means twenty percent is not. Flat headcount means the remainder cannot come from reductions. It has to come from the operation running better.

### The proof point BMO handed us

Levine, same Investor Day, on the Lexi chatbot: it *"truly automates the work of what used to be 28 FTEs on a call centre,"* automating frontline knowledge management across *"over 600 policy directives."*

BMO has already proven internally that automating contact-centre work converts to FTE-equivalent capacity, and it has a number for it. That is the single most useful sentence any prospect published this year, because it removes the entire "does this actually work" conversation before it starts.

### What BMO has disclosed about its own operations

- Canadian Personal & Business Banking serves clients through *"integrated branch, contact centre and digital channels."* (Annual Information Form, fiscal year ended October 31 2025.)
- **Call Assist**, in BMO's own words: *"A real-time, multilingual agent-assist tool that improves first-call resolution and reduces misdirected calls, enabling more focused and empathetic client interactions through streamlined workflows and integrated analytics."* Won Best Agent-Assist/Copilot Deployment for Contact Centre, announced May 26 2026.
- **SAFE** (Simplified Authorization for Entities), BMO's own words: *"A North American platform that enhances account signer management by using AI and machine learning to digitize, validate, and access records across the bank."* Same announcement.
- More than 53,000 full-time equivalent employees as of October 31 2025.
- Enterprise employee AI adoption *"over 96%"* (Investor Day, March 26 2026).

### Recent financial context (company-disclosed)

- Q1 FY2026 severance charge: $202 million pre-tax, $147 million after tax. Reported efficiency ratio 58.6%; excluding severance, adjusted 55.8%.
- Q2 FY2026: efficiency ratio 54.4%, positive operating leverage 4.1%. Net income $2,630 million reported.
- Darryl White, Q1 release: *"Our focus on closely managing expenses and operational efficiency is creating capacity for strategic investments."*
- White, Investor Day: *"We've delivered consistent operating leverage in nine out of the last 10 years."* And on AI: *"By fiscal 2030, we have line of sight to over a $1 billion of PPPT."*
- Sale of Transportation Finance and Vendor Finance expected to close Q4 FY2026 with an anticipated $1.1 billion pre-tax charge. This will dominate the Q4 narrative and is noise for our purposes.

### Calendar anchor

**BMO reports Q3 FY2026 on August 25 2026.** The quarter ended July 31. Nalgirkar will be asked on that call whether the $250 million program is on track, and it will be the third consecutive quarter he has had to answer for it.

**Fiscal year ends October 31 2026.** That is the deadline by which the first half of the $250 million must be banked, and FY2027 planning firms up in the weeks before it.

**The sequence is built to complete on Friday August 21 and go dark August 24 through 27.** Sending into an earnings print is a placement problem and a judgement problem. Q3 is also the natural re-entry point with fresh language for anyone who did not respond.
