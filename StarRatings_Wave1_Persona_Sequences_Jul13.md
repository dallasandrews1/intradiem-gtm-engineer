## Objection quick-handles (both lanes)

**"We already have WFM."** Right, and WFM tells you what should happen with staffing. This is about what happens the moment conditions change, the automated response between the forecast and the floor. It sits alongside WFM, it doesn't replace it.

**"We're already improving our Stars."** You are, and it shows. The contracts still under the line are the expensive part of that climb, where the gap opens under load and a scorecard can't close it after the fact. That's the piece we'd help hold, so the progress doesn't stall at the hardest contracts.

**"Wait for the October ratings."** The ratings in October report a measurement window that's closing now. Waiting means the next cliff is already set before anyone acts. The half-star moves during the year, not on release day.

**"Send me information."** Happy to. The one thing a deck won't answer is which of your contracts are closest to the line and most execution-sensitive, which is a fifteen-minute conversation. I'll send the overview and we can find time if it's worth it.
> *Optional verified proof point (compliant to add here, 1:1 blinded):* a healthcare plan we work with saw 4.5X annualized ROI in the first quarter and freed roughly 1,700 agent hours (intradiem.com, blinded). Use only in 1:1 replies, keep the customer blinded, never on a slide or blast.

**"Show me a plan whose Star Rating you actually moved."** Straight answer: I'm not going to hand you a Stars number I can't stand behind, and I'd be skeptical of anyone who did, since the rating moves on a dozen inputs. What I can show is the operational mechanism and what it returns: a healthcare plan we work with saw 4.5X annualized ROI in a quarter and freed roughly 1,700 agent hours (blinded). The reason for the fifteen minutes is to pressure-test whether that mechanism fits your specific measure gap, because if your gap is clinical rather than service, I'd tell you we're not the lever. That's worth knowing either way.

## Ops-lane decision and committee choreography

**Ops lane for Wave 1:** run the two buyer lanes above, Stars/Quality and Finance. Do NOT stand up a separate contact-center-operations lane for Wave 1. The operations contacts in the list (VP Operations, member-experience, contact-center ops) fold into the **Stars/Quality lane**, because at a plan chasing a half-star the operations leader and the Stars leader are hearing the same idea from their two angles: the last points live on the floor during peak weeks. The Centene strike-room sequence is the proof that the ops angle carries the Stars narrative well. Back-office operations (Segment 3 in the variant pack) stays gated on the Scott Kemme ICP conversation and is not part of Wave 1.

**Staggered committee entry per account:** do not fire both lanes at the same plan on Day 1. Stagger so a coherent half-star narrative surfaces inside the account within one week without looking like a blast. Enter the Stars/Quality contact on Day 1; enter the Finance contact one to two days behind. Where a plan has multiple committee contacts, keep the bodies and CTAs distinct per person so two of them side by side see different angles on the same problem, not one template with a swapped name. Where an email is at deliverability risk, let the LinkedIn touch carry the opener so the thread doesn't go cold.
