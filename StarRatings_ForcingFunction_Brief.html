Star Ratings Motion · Forcing Function Brief · for the outbound team

What this motion is really about, and what every message must earn its way to

The one page to read before writing a Star Ratings message. It names the pressure the prospect actually feels, who feels it, the slice of the problem we can honestly speak to, and the hard line on what we may and may not claim.

01

The forcing function

A Medicare Advantage plan below 4.0 Stars forgoes its CMS Quality Bonus Payment. That is real, recurring money tied directly to the rating, and the gap between a 3.5-star contract and the 4.0 bonus line is a half-star. The plan feels it across the org, and leadership is running to close it before the next cycle locks. Our job is to reach the people who own that number with a reason it matters right now.
02

Why it bites now

CMS resets Star Ratings every October, so the window is annual and unforgiving. On the current 2026-cycle cut, the sharpest targets are the cliff-edge contracts sitting at exactly 3.5 stars, one half-star under the bonus line, where a small operational move on the addressable measures can flip the payment. Plans further back sit at 3.0 and are a longer-horizon play; we say so honestly rather than manufacture a same-cycle cliff. Every figure we use here is an estimate from public CMS data, labeled as such, because the prospect knows their exact picture better than we do.

03

Who feels it, and the angle for each

Owns the number

Chief Medicare Officer · VP Stars / Quality

The rating is their scorecard. A half-star slip is their name on the miss, and they are accountable for the improvement plan.

Message angleLead with the specific measures inside their control and a credible path to move them before the cycle closes.
Feels the money

CFO · VP Finance

Sees the forgone bonus as a line item. Responds to the size of the exposure framed against something they already track, not to feature talk.

Message angleQuantify the exposure as a public-data estimate, then connect closing it to an operational lever, never a vague promise.
Operates in it

VP / Dir Contact Center · Member Services · Ops

Owns the customer-service and admin measures that actually move Stars. Feels the staffing, adherence, and backlog pressure every day.

Message angleSpeak to the operational measures directly; this is where Queue Optimizer and the workforce-orchestration story land hardest.
04

The wedge, stated honestly

Not all of a plan's Star gap is ours to move. The winnable slice is the operational and customer-service-addressable measures. We anchor there and let the prospect route us to the rest. Claiming the whole Star gap is where credibility dies.

Where we speak · addressable
  • The still-scoring customer-service measures: CAHPS customer service, complaints, disenrollment. Not the retired Call Center Monitoring secret-shopper measure.
  • Administrative and access measures driven by ops execution
  • Schedule adherence, shrinkage, and backlog that operations controls
  • The Queue Optimizer and Dynamic Workforce Orchestration angle
Where we do not overreach
  • Clinical and HEDIS measures we do not move
  • Pharmacy and medication-adherence clinical outcomes
  • Anything implying we lift the full Star rating on our own
  • Measures already retired or changing in the next cycle, including the Call Center Monitoring measure, gone for 2028 Stars
05 · The hard line

What we may claim, and what we may not

Cleared to send
  • Star levels, forgone-QBP estimates, and cliff counts, always labeled "estimate from public CMS data."
  • The UnitedHealthcare $190M figure as UHC's own stated court claim, under the prior rules, historical proof only. Never imply moving that measure still earns bonus; it is retired for 2028 Stars.
  • Intradiem sells Dynamic Workforce Orchestration across contact centers and back offices, six verticals.
Do not send
  • The Humana story, or any named or blind customer outcome. No verified Intradiem customer result exists in the Value Repository yet, so it does not ship.
  • Any idle-time percentage, or any Intradiem ROI, NRR, or performance metric. All unsourced placeholders.
  • Intercom or Rippling proof points. Other vendors' results, never ours to claim.
  • The stale "157 Director+ contacts" and "38 sub-4.0 plans" counts. Prior-cycle numbers, superseded.
  • Profit or revenue percentages we cannot defend from public filings on that specific plan.
Source of truth: Intradiem Value Repository. A claim not in that file does not reach a prospect. When a verified customer story is approved, it gets added there first, then it can lead.
