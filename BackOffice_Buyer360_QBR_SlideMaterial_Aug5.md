## The spine: four personas, constant across every vertical

The slide's trick: the PERSONAS never change, only the faces and titles do. Reps know this org chart cold in the front office; this is the back-office translation.

| Persona | Role in the deal | What changes by industry |
|---|---|---|
| **1. COO / VP Operations** | Economic buyer. Owns SLA, cost-to-serve, and regulatory exposure. Can greenlight or veto alone | Which division COO actually owns the back office |
| **2. WFM / Resource Planning** | Technical evaluator and champion. Lives in forecasting, scheduling, intra-day orchestration | Rarely called "WFM" in the back office; shows up as workforce planning, resource planning, operations planning |
| **3. Head of Operations / Ops Director** | Day-to-day champion. Manages breach rates, escalations, team burnout. Their lives get better | The function name: claims, disputes, billing, order management, enrollment, case processing |
| **4. IT / CTO** | Gatekeeper. Approves integrations, security, data residency. Gates any AI purchase; matters double for BOO with multiple LLMs inside | Which platforms they protect (core banking, claims systems, OSS/BSS, metering) |

Tags: [IB] = install-base (front office is already an Intradiem customer, this is expansion). [NN] = net-new logo.

## Slide concept ideas (for the interactive build)

1. **Fixed spine, swappable faces.** The four personas are a permanent vertical spine; clicking a vertical's card swaps the four faces, titles, and logos. The message lands on repetition: the buyer is always these four people, only their titles change costumes per industry.
2. **The translation row.** Under each face, one line: "front office calls this ___, back office calls it ___". That is the exact knowledge gap the slide erases; reps know the front-office org chart cold and are blind here.
3. **Install-base badge.** A badge on [IB] faces: "we already own their front office." First click tells reps most of this slide is expansion into buildings we are already inside, not cold prospecting.
4. **The two aha moments to engineer.** First: "now I know exactly who to look for in every vertical." Second: "GTM has already found them, automatically, with sequences ready; I should run campaigns through that engine." The slide is itself the proof of the function.
5. **Findability cue.** Subtle icon per persona: the COO is the hard find, the WFM-equivalent is the aliased find, the Ops Director is the volume find, the gatekeeper is the must-find-early. That is the BDR nuance in one glance.
6. **Government card.** Grayed-out sixth card labeled "activates later" so the framework reads complete without implying work that hasn't happened.
