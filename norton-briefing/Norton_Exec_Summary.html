The job to be done

The Star Ratings window is live and it moves weekly: CMS releases, filings language, quality leadership changes, hiring clusters. Working it properly means 32 parent organizations at committee depth, roughly five seats per parent with a distinct angle each, five touches per seat across channels. That is hundreds of researched, personal touches per full pass, at a pace signals set rather than calendars. It is a systems problem, and building the system that solves it is what GTM engineering is for.

What runs today

I run a live GTM engine in Clay. The 2026 CMS Star Ratings universe is scored and graded live on native formulas: 307 contracts, 98 Tier A and B, 32 parent organizations. A three-persona buying-committee pull, Stars and Quality leaders, Medicare-segment finance including Chief Actuaries, and contact-center and member-services operations, filled the contacts table with 143 verified decision-makers across all 32 target parents. Current customers are locked out of every send by the engine itself, the sync that feeds a campaign refuses any row at a customer parent, and those rows are held for the expansion lane instead. Every loaded lead carries a validated work email and sits behind a human-approval send gate. Two persona campaigns hold Wave 1, 38 leads (23 Stars/Quality, 15 Finance), with a 15-lead longer-horizon lane staged behind them, in draft behind per-lead human review, a deliberately small calibration wave that turns planning ranges into our own numbers before volume ships. Nothing has gone out yet; that is the gates doing their job, not a delay.

The meeting

Thirty minutes, engine live on screen, with everything the engine needs already in place: Salesforce data flows through the data hub AI Enablement runs, reporting runs through the Greenlight Salesforce agents, and the tooling lives inside the approved stack. Results run on the ranges already in your inbox, 6 to 11 replies, 3 to 7 qualified conversations, 2 to 4 meetings on the full motion, with engine results feeding a Salesforce dashboard you can open any time, shaped for pipeline council, nothing new to log into. Spend follows the evidence: where replies show traction, the motion doubles down, and every lane is continuously audited. The one decision on the table is the start: approve Wave 1, and your read on which accounts go first shapes where it points. Total build cost to date (as of Jul 15) is about 820 Clay credits, roughly $57 of research (credits, not dollars: about 7 cents each), logged line by line in an append-only ledger you can audit. This is a head start on a build that compounds weekly; the continuous engineering is the role.
