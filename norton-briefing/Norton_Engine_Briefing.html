02 · The market it points at

The accounts in that window carry nine-figure Stars money.

Every parent in the target universe is scored against the 2026 CMS release, with forgone quality-bonus dollars estimated from public CMS data and enrollment. Estimates, clearly labeled; each plan knows its exact picture better than we do. Two examples already graded and staffed with committee contacts:

$61.2M est.

Medica estimated forgone QBP, 3 cliff-edge contracts on the 2026 cycle. Committee contacts already in the table.

The window in plain terms: what these plans do in the 2026 and 2027 measurement years is what the 2028 Star Ratings will grade, and the call-center measures we credibly move still count in that window before CMS retires them. Forgone bonus is forgone revenue, dollars that flow straight into the plan's top line when a contract clears 4.0.

07 · How we measure

Attribution set on day one. Reported where you already look.

Reply → qualified → meeting

Every contact is tracked from first send through reply, qualified conversation, and meeting booked. Qualified means a target persona engaging on the problem; auto-replies, referrals, and polite declines do not count. Attribution is set the day a contact enters the system, not reconstructed later.

The activity layer

Sends, deliverability, and reply rates by persona ride the same readout, so a quiet week reads as a targeting problem or a deliverability problem, never a mystery.

Cost per qualified reply

Every enrichment credit is logged against the motion that spent it, so spend ties directly to output. The unit economics of the engine, reviewable any week.

Sourced vs. existing, never blended

Engine-sourced replies, meetings, and pipeline report separately from anything already in motion, and estimated opportunity never blends with realized results. The engine only gets credit for what it actually created.

Reporting lives where you already plan: engine results feed Salesforce directly, with actuals against these ranges in a dashboard you can open any time, shaped to how pipeline council and the H1 and H2 reviews already look at pipeline. Nothing new to log into.

08 · Signals, priced before they run

The list keeps itself alive. Half of that costs nothing.

Free · computed from data already in the engine

The cliff-edge signal. Fires points when a contract sits just under 4.0 and self-cleans to zero the moment it clears the line, so an account that solves its problem drops off the list the same day. Intent rollup, fire dates, and the tier logic (Tier 1 fires outreach, Tier 2 raises priority) run the same way: native formulas, zero credits, the most important signal for the cliff play.

Paid · enrichments, pre-estimated per run

The outside world. Stars language in SEC filings, earnings-call mentions, new quality leadership, quality hiring clusters, backlog news. Each is an enrichment per parent, roughly 2.5 credits per signal, so a full five-signal sweep across all 32 parents runs about 400 credits, logged in the ledger before it fires like everything else.

09 · Inside the workbook

Three things to see in the system.

In the workbook

The living list

Scoring reacts to data: contacts land and the account's grade recomputes, and the cliff-edge signal zeroes itself the moment a contract clears 4.0, so a plan that solves its problem drops off the list the same day. Not a report, a living list.

In the workbook

The gate rail

Every row carries its whole gate on one screen: the automated critic's verdict, the human approval, the send-ready state. The sync that feeds a campaign refuses any row missing one of them, and all three campaigns hold in draft until a person deliberately launches.

In the workbook

Collision control

One checkbox: a rep claims a contact, the engine stands down on that person and their whole company, outranking any approval. A reply stops that contact's sequence on its own; the company-wide stand-down is one click behind it.

The tables stay open the whole session, and anything you want to see is one click away. And whatever this room decides it wants eyes on becomes a standing readout that finds you; the engine's job is to bring you answers, not another tool to log into.

10 · For the revenue org

A wave a week without new headcount. Every number traces to its source.

A weekly motion, by default

Templates, scoring, gates, and the ledger operate as one repeatable cycle: a full wave ships every week, and human judgment concentrates in the approval gate, where it decides what reaches a prospect instead of being spent on mechanical work. The wave itself spends nothing; sending is free, credits price research, and that research is already inside the steady state.

Pipeline you can trace to its source

Every contact is stamped with its source motion and sourced date at creation. Surfaced opportunity reports separately from realized, never blended, so when a number reaches your forecast you can walk it back to the row that produced it.

11 · For marketing

Marketing fires on the same signal, at the same moment.

Triggered campaigns

One account signal produces three synchronized outputs: a campaign brief for marketing, an alert for the owning seller, and a gated outreach draft. Marketing and sales act on the same trigger at the same time instead of finding out from each other later.

The content engine

One source asset, a webinar, a customer story, a conference talk, becomes a coordinated drop: blog post, LinkedIn set, nurture sequence, sales one-pager, all consistent and on brand in one pass. Volume without the team growing.

This engine is built as a technology partner to marketing, not a parallel motion, and the near-term prize is the story vault: two decades of customers who stayed, with story after story still to tell. Any one of them, run through the engine, becomes a full coordinated drop with every claim tied to a verified source, and the engine hands marketing more surface area on triggers no one could watch manually.

12 · For sales

Meetings arrive pre-scored. Reps never collide with the engine.

Committee context attached

Before the first call, the seller has the account grade, tier, the estimated Stars dollars at stake, and which committee members are already engaged. The meeting starts at the second conversation, not the first.

One contact, one owner

A reply stops that contact's sequence on the spot, and one claim checkbox stands the engine down on that person and their whole company. From that moment the contact belongs to a human. No double-touches, ever, by construction.
