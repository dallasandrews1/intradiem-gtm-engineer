# First Two Weeks: Stakeholder Meeting Plan

This converts the strategy into motion. The goal of your first two weeks is not to
deploy anything. It is four outcomes: orient your roadmap to the CRO's number, recruit
at least one real co-owner, fill in the dependency map with real timelines, and start
the one motion that needs nobody's permission.

## The meta-rule

Ask for input and partnership, not access. People grant access to things they helped
shape and defend things they had no hand in. Every meeting below is framed to make the
owner an author of the outcome, not a gate in front of it. You already won the build.
These two weeks are political, not technical.

## Your starting advantage

You already know half of these people from the interview loop, and they already vetted
and liked you. Genna owns Sales Ops, Nathan owns pipeline development, Cheryl owns
product marketing, Naveen hired you, Busbee is your product exec. Those are warm
relationships, not cold asks. The genuinely new relationships are the CRO, the data and
IT owners, and procurement. Spend your fresh political capital there.

## Week 1: orient, listen, secure air cover

**Naveen (Director Product Strategy, your sponsor) — Day 1.**
Ask: the org map and where he sees the biggest gap. Framing: "You hired this role to build a motion from scratch. Walk me through the gap you most want closed and who owns the pieces I'll need, so I build with the org instead of around it." Yes looks like: he gives you the real owner list and warm intros.

**John Norton (CRO) — early week 1, ideally via Naveen.**
This is the most important meeting of the two weeks. Ask: his number-one GTM priority. Framing: "Before I build anything, I want my roadmap pointed at your number. In two quarters, which would move the needle more for you, net-new pipeline or systematizing expansion?" Yes looks like: he names the priority and becomes your air cover. Watch-out: do not pitch your system here. Ask and listen. You are recruiting a sponsor, not demoing.

**Chris Busbee (SVP Product) — week 1.**
Ask: a partnership on the product data the modernization exposed. Framing: "The new platform exposes event data that could power expansion signals. I want to use it responsibly, behind your controls. Can we co-design what is safe to access and how?" Yes looks like: he opens the product-data door and owns the governance with you.

**Genna Barrett-Moeller (Director Global Sales Ops) — week 1.**
Ask: co-ownership of anything touching Salesforce. Framing: "Your ops backbone is what makes any of this real. I will not put anything near Salesforce without you shaping it. Will you co-own the data model with me, read-only first?" Yes looks like: she becomes a co-owner instead of a gatekeeper. Lean on the rapport you already built with her.

## Week 2: recruit co-owners on the longer poles, start the parallel tracks

**Nathan Belfield (Pipeline Development Lead) — start of week 2.**
Ask: be your first pilot rep. Framing: "You know which signals are real and which are noise better than I will. I want to pilot the net-new plays with you first, your calibration and my infrastructure. Will you be rep number one?" Yes looks like: your pilot is live and the person with the most scope overlap is now a partner, not a rival.

**Cheryl Eckel (Lead PMM) plus Legal or Compliance — week 2.**
Ask: build the claim-approval workflow and verified proof points with you. Framing: "Before any AI-drafted outreach reaches a prospect, I need your guardrails. Help me build the approval path and the verified numbers." Yes looks like: marketing co-owns the messaging governance, which both neutralizes the compliance risk and stops them feeling bypassed.

**CTO org / data engineering (Kevin Wilson's team) — week 2.**
Ask: a safe, governed way to read product-usage data. Framing: "I need a governed way to read usage data that never leaves your controls. Help me design it so it is safe by construction." Yes looks like: the data-governance review starts with them as a partner, not a blocker.

**IT / Slack workspace admin — week 2.**
Ask: plan the future Slack review now. Framing: "The Claude plugin needs nothing from you. When we want Slack, here is exactly what it would need. Let us plan the review now so it is never a surprise." Yes looks like: no shadow-IT reaction later because you brought them in first.

**Procurement / InfoSec — week 2 (start the long pole).**
Ask: begin the vendor review for the two enrichment tools. Framing: "I want to start the security and procurement review for Clay and Apollo now, because I know it takes time. What do you need from me to begin?" Yes looks like: the longest-lead clock is already running while you ship value elsewhere.

## The two tracks to start immediately

1. **Net-new pilot with Nathan**, using external data only. This needs no internal access and is your visible wedge. Start it the moment Nathan says yes.
2. **Procurement for Clay and Apollo.** The longest pole. Open it in week one even though you will not use it for weeks, so it is not the thing that stalls month three.

## End-of-two-weeks checkpoint

You should be able to say all of these:

- You know the CRO's top priority and have his air cover.
- At least one named co-owner has committed (most likely Genna, Nathan, or Busbee).
- The dependency map is filled in with real owners and honest timelines.
- The net-new pilot is underway with Nathan on external data.
- The procurement and data-governance reviews are both in motion.
- The claim-approval workflow conversation has started with Cheryl and legal.

If those are true, you have done the one thing the premortem said matters most: you
walked in recruiting co-owners, not presenting a finished system. Everything after this
is execution on ground you have already made friendly.
