03 · Lane one

Insurance and FS: the £680 meter is already running

Every complaint that reaches the Financial Ombudsman costs the firm £680, win or lose, and the volumes get published twice a year with the firm's name on them. That's the same structural shape as the Stars cliff edge in the US motion: a public number with a price tag attached, landing on the exact surfaces Intradiem touches.

£680
Per ombudsman case in 2026/27, upheld or not. The ombudsman's published fee, not our number.
+87.8%
NatWest's addressable complaint growth, H2 over H1 2025, from the FOS's own firm-level data.
£4.71M
NatWest's half-year fee exposure at the current rate. An estimate against published volumes, labeled that way in copy.

The universe came from the regulator's list, not a firmographic guess: 235 business groups publishing complaint data, cut to 52 whose complaint drivers plausibly move with workforce orchestration. Handling delays, capacity, coaching gaps. Firms whose complaints are driven by pricing or product design got dropped no matter how bad their number looks, because we can't honestly help them. The mid-sequence email runs on the second public hook, the eight-week referral rule.

Committee: five seats per firm, Director and above. Head of Complaints is the sharpest entry because they own the published number and the per-case fee personally. COO is the economic buyer, and the seat that shortens an 18-month cycle when engaged early.

04 · Lane two

Airlines: a legal clock that starts at three hours

Land three or more hours late for a reason within the airline's control and every passenger is owed a fixed payment under UK261: £220, £350 or £520 depending on distance, claimable for up to six years. One delayed A320 at ~180 seats is a five-figure exposure event on a single rotation. And when disruption hits, the compensation is only the visible cost. The claims backlog, the contact-centre surge, and the re-accommodation queue that follow are all workforce surfaces.

37,659
Flights on 24 July 2026, the all-time European traffic record (Eurocontrol week 30).
1 in 5
Flights held by flow restrictions that same week. More than one in four arrived late.
41
Wave-1 contacts already carrying reviewed, personalised copy variables, ready to load.

Twelve accounts graded, six passenger airlines in wave 1 with easyJet as the sprint account, ground handlers staged behind them with their own copy spine, because their exposure is contractual SLA, not passenger compensation. One honest gap: TUI belongs in this universe and its committee wouldn't resolve through Clay, so it gets sourced separately before wave 2.

The timing is deliberate. Airlines does not start in August. Ops and ground seats are in peak firefighting and nothing lands. Start is staged for late August at the earliest, so calls and the direct back half of the sequence arrive in September, right after peak, while the summer's claims backlog is still arriving and the pain is fresh. Insurance has no season, so it goes first.

05 · The sequence

Jack's cadence, wired to the last branch

This is the sprint Jack described on our call, systematised. Ten days, multichannel, two accounts in flight at a time, and every path ends in a reply, a meeting, or a clean exit. Both campaigns run the same tree with lane-specific copy.

Day 1
Email, then LinkedIn profile visit, then a note-less connection request. All three the same day; the visit is what makes the invite land.
Day 2
Call, leave a voicemail. It references the email's subject and names Jack at the end, never the opening. Add value, get out.
Then it branches
On whether the invite is accepted within two days, and on whether we hold a phone number.
Invite accepted
  1. DM in Jack's own line: "shot you a note via email, thought LinkedIn might be quicker" (manual approve)
  2. Voice note (manual, Jack's voice)
  3. Second call
  4. Mid-sequence email, new angle on the same idea
  5. Day 10: "closing the file"
Not accepted · phone held
  1. Call
  2. WhatsApp touch (manual approve)
  3. Follow-up call
  4. Mid-sequence email
  5. WhatsApp nudge (manual approve)
  6. Breakup
Not accepted · no phone
  1. Like + follow + profile visit
  2. Mid-sequence email
  3. Breakup

Details that matter: every email carries an opt-out line and the breakup says plainly it's the last note. Sign-off runs "Jack" through day 5 and "Jack Ohagan" from day 6. A reply anywhere stops everything, instantly. And the copy carries zero Intradiem statistics; every number in it belongs to a regulator and is cited that way.

07 · The rails

What makes it safe to put Jack's name on automation

The exclusion check is a hard gate

The UK customer list is in and already applied: AXA's contacts are excluded from the universe. The check still reruns on real rows at every load, including legal-name variants and subsidiaries. No exceptions, no synthetic shortcuts.

Claims discipline

Zero Intradiem statistics in this copy. Every figure belongs to the FOS, the CAA or Eurocontrol and is cited as theirs. The two UK insurer customers stay unnamed everywhere until clearance says otherwise.

UK solicitation rules, respected

Opt-out language on every email, sends bucketed by account, never blasted. Spray and pray is a legal exposure here, not just a deliverability problem, so it doesn't exist in this motion.

Nothing starts by itself

Both campaigns sit in draft until an explicit start. Two accounts in flight at a time, waves loaded deliberately, and the airlines start window is calendar-staged, not automatic.
