05 · The sequence

Adaptive trees, not blasts

None of the three campaigns is a linear drip. Each one branches on what the prospect actually does, so nobody gets a call task for a lead with no phone number and nobody gets a cold opener after they've opened three emails.

Invite accepted
  1. The conversation moves to LinkedIn (every send manual-approve)
  2. Call task backs it up
  3. Close or clean exit
Phone number held
  1. Call task enters the cadence
  2. Email and LinkedIn continue around it
  3. Breakup stays warm or cold based on engagement
Opened but never replied
  1. Resurrection only: warm re-opener referencing the earlier thread
  2. Never-opened contacts get the cold breakup instead

Details that matter: a reply anywhere stops the lead and the whole company, instantly. Click tracking is off to protect deliverability. Every LinkedIn touch is manual-approve, so nothing ships in Nate's name without Nate's hand. And the copy carries zero Intradiem statistics; the numbers belong to CMS and the public record, cited that way.

07 · The rails

What makes it safe to put Nate's name on automation

Every load is hand-verified against the 101-account customer file, including the alias traps that string-matching misses: legal names, subsidiaries and recent acquisitions get checked before anything else runs. Zero hits so far, and the check reruns at every load.

Claims discipline

Zero Intradiem statistics in this copy. Every number belongs to CMS or the public record and is cited as theirs. Anything that couldn't be verified at the primary source stayed out.

Deliverability protected

Click tracking off, weekday-only sending inside business hours, daily caps shared with Nate's mailbox warmth budget. The sending reputation being protected is Nate's.

Nothing starts by itself
