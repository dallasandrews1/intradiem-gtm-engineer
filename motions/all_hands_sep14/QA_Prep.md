# All-hands Q&A prep

Ten questions the room is most likely to ask, with the answer to give out loud. Tone: confident, factual, understated. Never mention outages, fixes, or caught bugs. Automation-first framing: checkpoints are an aperture that widens, not a wall.

**1. What tools is this built on?**
Claude Code is the brain: it runs the agents, the scoring engines, and writes the drafts. Clay is the data layer: contacts, enrichment, and workflows. lemlist runs the multichannel sends. Salesforce stays the system of record; everything is counted there like any other channel.

**2. Does the AI send emails by itself?**
The engine composes, sequences, and executes at scale, with judgment checkpoints tuned per motion. Early motions get tight checkpoints; as a motion earns trust, the aperture widens. Two things are hard-gated everywhere: current customers never enter a cold campaign, and only verified company numbers appear in outreach.

**3. How do you keep AI-written copy accurate and on brand?**
Every claim in outreach has to exist in our verified value repository with a source, or it doesn't go in. Drafts are written in the sending rep's voice and pass a copy gate before they're loaded. The AI writes the first draft; the standard it writes to is ours.

**4. Could my team use something like this?**
Yes, and it already happens. When account management needed back-office maps for twelve strategic accounts, the engine sourced the people, checked them against Salesforce, and delivered the maps as a live page the AMs work from. The engine is built as self-serve skills teams run themselves, not requests that queue behind me. If your team has a repetitive workflow, come find me; that's also the AI Champion lane we're building with the product team.

**5. What did this cost?**
It runs on a metered budget, and every motion is costed before it runs. The database and segmentation work you saw runs at zero marginal cost; enrichment is metered per contact. The spend conversation happens before a run, never after.

**6. How long did it take to build?**
The foundation started before day one, and it's been a compounding build since July. Each new motion now stamps from the last one in minutes; that's the point of building an engine instead of one-off campaigns.

**7. Is this replacing salespeople?**
No. It removes the sourcing, research, and first-draft work that kept sellers out of conversations. Reps own every send, every reply, and every meeting. The engine's job is that a rep's first hour of the day starts with a warm list and drafts, not a blank screen.

**8. What about data privacy and security?**
Company data stays inside the tools the company already controls: Salesforce, Clay, lemlist, Slack. The agents read, log, and route internally. New AI tools go through the contracts-team review we set up on the product side.

**9. How do you know it's working?**
Every motion is measured on cost per meeting against the same baseline as our other channels, in Salesforce, on the same dashboard leadership already reads. Estimated pipeline and realized results are never blended; realized only counts when it lands in the CRM.

**10. What's next?**
Back-office expansion sequences inside our customer base go live around this all-hands, with AM clearance on every name. Then intent-triggered outbound later in September, partner campaigns with Haresh's team, and the October Star Ratings wave the morning CMS publishes.

**11. Why buying committees? We've always sold to a person.**
Because the deal never sits with one person. Every target account now has four named roles: the economic buyer who owns the cost line, the evaluator who owns the workforce or planning function, the champion whose day gets better, and the integration owner who has to say yes to the systems. The engine finds all four per account, across every product and industry we sell into, and the sequences reach all four. That's what changed the conversation with sales and with Matt.

**12. What's the difference between this and AI enablement?**
Same direction, different lane. AI enablement, with Tyler and Jen, is about every team getting the tools and the skills. GTM Engineering is one team running its whole process on them, end to end, and handing the working pieces back as shared skills anyone can run. We're the worked example.

**13. Where does the time actually go back? (per act, if pressed)**
- Act 1: an account plan with the committee and a ten-day multi-channel sequence used to be a day of research and drafting per account by hand; it is now one ask and a few minutes. Estimate, and say so.
- Act 2: a back-office org map for one account was a week of LinkedIn and spreadsheet time for an AM; eighteen accounts were mapped and checked against Salesforce in days. Estimate, and say so.
- Act 3: five touches across three channels for every person in a campaign of 40 to 50 leads is 200-plus drafts and a calendar of steps per rep; the rep now reviews and owns replies. The 370-hours figure below is the conservative whole.

**14. How do you know which platforms a company runs?**
From their own job posts. The engine reads what a company has been hiring for and returns each contact center and workforce platform with the date it was last seen, one credit per company. A vendor name only goes into a rep's email when it was seen in the last twelve months; anything older is context for the rep and the copy stays generic. We don't scan their website for this, because a contact center platform never shows up there.

## The slide numbers, if pressed

The bases stay off the slides and in your pocket:
- **1,800 hours** (problem slide) = 4,400 contacts (the count when the estimate was made; 4,600 today) at roughly 25 minutes each to find, verify, research, and write five touches by hand.
- **370 hours** (results slide) = 4,400 contacts at 5 minutes each of sourcing and research alone, the narrower, most conservative cut.
Both are estimates and you say so if asked. The spoken frame is capacity, never headcount: by hand this work never happens, and the hours the engine does go back to sellers as conversation time.

## If asked something outside these

Answer what's true today in one sentence, then: "Grab me after, I'll show you the real thing." Never speculate about roadmap commitments, other teams' tools, or vendor decisions (6sense stays out of every answer).
