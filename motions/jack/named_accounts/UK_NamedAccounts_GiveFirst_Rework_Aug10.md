### Note 9. Sky, coming through a long integration with the team intact

For Mottram and Rankin. Anchor: NBCUniversal spin plus ITV acquisition, both multi-year.

> Subject: as promised
>
> Hi {{firstName}}, as promised. What I see separating the operations teams that come through a long integration with their people intact from the ones that get consolidated:
>
> 1. The absorption gets evidenced while it happens. Both processes running over Sky now are multi-year, which means the operation carries the uncertainty for a long time before any structure is decided. The teams that keep their people are the ones who can show what their operation absorbed, hour by hour, at the time. Reconstructed afterwards from a report, it never carries the same weight.
>
> 2. "Duplicated" gets answered with the day, not the org chart. Integration reviews look for duplicated operations on paper. Two teams with similar names look duplicated; two teams whose intraday reality is visibly different don't. The evidence for that difference only exists if it was being captured while the days were running.
>
> 3. The quiet windows fund the resilience. Long integrations stretch people thin in bursts and leave slack in pockets. The teams that hold their service line through it are the ones using those pockets for the coaching, training and callbacks that otherwise become next quarter's problem.
>
> If any of that maps to how live operations is running through this at Sky, glad to go one level deeper. If not, it's yours to keep either way.
>
> Jack

### Note 10. British Airways, Nimbus and the co-existence window

For Bradley Tan only. Anchor: Nimbus live, FCR protection through the transition.

> Subject: as promised
>
> Hi Bradley, as promised. The short version of where the FCR risk in a migration like Nimbus actually sits, from what I see across contact centre transitions:
>
> 1. The dip lives in the co-existence window, not the platform. For the months old and new run together, the agent decides which process applies to the call in front of them. First contact resolution goes there, not to the technology, and it recovers on how fast those decision points get found and closed, not on the cutover date.
>
> 2. Knowledge readiness moves week to week once you're live. The gap between what the process says and what agents actually do opens widest mid-migration and shifts as each wave of customers moves. Teams that treat readiness as a launch task get surprised; teams that treat it as a running number don't.
>
> 3. The recovery runs on triggers, not reports. The queues, teams and call types where the new process isn't landing are visible inside the day they slip. Acting in that window keeps the transition out of the customer numbers; the weekly report version of the same information mostly documents the damage.
>
> If any of that maps to what you're seeing inside Nimbus, glad to go one level deeper on the specifics. If not, it's yours to keep either way.
>
> Jack
