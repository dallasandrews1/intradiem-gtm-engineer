## What AI means for UK outbound, and for my role

**Where we are today.** UK demand generation is one person: me. My quarterly target is 10 meetings, quality over quantity, and I hit it through LinkedIn and email done by hand, account by account. That works, but it caps out. Every hour I spend assembling a sequence is an hour I'm not using the thing that actually books meetings here, which is knowing this market.

**What's already running.** Working with Dallas, we've got a head start most teams would need a quarter to build. Two UK campaigns are built and sitting ready (insurance/FS and airlines): 10-day multi-channel sprints across email, LinkedIn, phone, voicemail, voice notes and WhatsApp, with every single lead carrying its own personalised lines, not a template with a name swapped in. When I told Dallas on Monday that a meeting ask in a first email won't work in this market, the messaging across every campaign changed the same day, and the same fix is now being A/B tested on the US side. That's the loop I want us to be known for: UK market judgment goes in, the whole system updates in hours.

**What it means for the UK team.** Three things change:

1. **Scale without spray.** We go from tens of touches a week to thousands, without becoming spam. The system enforces the things we'd want a careful human to do every time: opt-out lines on every email, current customers excluded before anything loads, every claim checked before it's allowed into copy, and nothing sends without a human decision.
2. **Answers in 10 days, not quarters.** Each campaign is a 10-day test. A vertical either produces conversations or it doesn't, and we know inside two weeks, then move to the next idea on the ranked list. We stop guessing which verticals are worth a quarter of effort.
3. **Everything in the open.** The work runs through a shared Slack channel: what went out, who replied, what was said, with a suggested response waiting when a reply lands. Matt and Simon can see the motion working and steer it, instead of finding out at the end of the month.

**What it means for my role.** I stop being the person who does the sending and become the person who makes the sending right. My job becomes the parts AI can't do here: knowing that Netherlands buyers want the ask up front, that water utilities buy on five-year cycles, which accounts we must never touch, and what a good meeting actually looks like for the AE it's booked for. My ambition, said plainly: double my output this year on the same hours.

**What I need to make it real: a Lemlist seat of my own.** The engine builds my campaigns, but I need to operate inside them daily (approving LinkedIn steps, sending voice notes, handling replies), and I want to build with it myself, not just receive. First build on my list: pre- and post-event follow-up sequences, so every event we attend gets a proper before-and-after motion instead of a business-card pile. That's a use case nobody has to design for me; I just need the license to run it. The ask on the table is three seats (Dallas, me, Nate), and mine is the one that turns the UK build from something done for me into something I run.

**Proof it's not theoretical.** Last week I booked two meetings off campaigns I ran myself, in a lane I was once told doesn't work. That's me at hand-speed. The point of this motion is what that looks like at system-speed, and the UK is set up to be the first team that finds out.
