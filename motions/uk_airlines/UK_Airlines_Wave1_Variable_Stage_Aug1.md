## Objection quick-handles (added v1.4, spoken form, verified figures only)

The only permitted source for airlines pushback blocks in the Daily Action Brief. Universal branches (busy, brush-off, "send me an email", "what's this about") live in motions/shared/Cold_Call_Playbook_Aug2.md; these are the motion-specific ones. Claims and mechanism never strengthen in rendering.

**"We've got a claims handling provider."** Right, and keep them. They handle the claim after it exists, pricing it, processing it. The part I'm on about happens earlier, in the hours around the three hour mark and the morning after, when the workforce either reshapes in time or the delay crosses the line and the claim gets born. Your provider handles the bill. This is about the operation that decides how many bills there are.

**"Our planning and WFM tools already cover this."** Right, and keep them. Planning sets what's supposed to happen. This is about the moment the day breaks the plan, the response while it's still happening. It sits alongside the planning stack, it doesn't replace it.

**"That's an ops problem, not mine."** Fair, the delay itself is ops. The morning after is yours though. The claims, the calls and the re-bookings all land on your team at once, and how bad that morning gets was mostly decided by staffing calls made the night before. That's the bit I wanted to talk through.

**"Call me after the summer."** Happy to, and I will. One thing worth knowing before then: a passenger has six years to claim, so every disrupted day between now and that call stays on the books either way, and the winter conversation is really about how next summer runs. Fifteen minutes now is the cheap version of that conversation.
