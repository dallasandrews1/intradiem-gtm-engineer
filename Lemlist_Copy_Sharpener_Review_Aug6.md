## Ranked fixes

### 1. The Resurrection breakup is verbatim deja vu (highest value, fix before launch)

Resurrection prospects are by definition people who already finished a fresh-pool sequence. The Resurrection "leaving it here" email (seq_3FpmiTeBzmnZiTzFy step 1, and seq_WbTtwaYkMggGu9ztX step 3) is the same copy as the Quality-lane breakup they already received, including "this is my last note." Saying "this is my last note" a second time, weeks after the first, tells the prospect the first one was theater.

Replacement draft (self-aware, earns the second pass):

> Subject: eating my words
>
> Hi {{firstName}}, I told you my last note was my last note, and then the scoring window kept shrinking, so here I am eating my words once. The measures still movable are being scored in live interactions right now, and once the window closes the cheap fixes close with it. If the last half-star makes the priority list before then, I'm easy to find.
>
> This one really is the last one.
>
> Nathan Belfield

Same logic for the opt-out line: resurrection prospects have already seen "If you'd rather not hear from me, one word and I'll stop" two or three times. In the "just tried you" email, vary it: "And if the answer's just no, one word does it."

### 2. The "not X, it's Y" contrast structure is the account's fingerprint

Instances a single prospect reads back to back: "rarely the delay itself, it's the recovery" / "a timing problem, not a people problem" / "not a caseworker problem" / "mostly operational, not clinical" / "an execution question, not a clinical one" / "decided weeks before the deadline, not at it" / "in real time rather than after the report" / plus "not on the October release" in nearly every Stars touch. One per email is a sharp writer. One per sentence across six touches is a rhetorical machine, and it is the closest thing here to an AI tell.

Fix: keep the single best contrast per email, rewrite the second one as a plain assertion. Example, "six years" email middle:

- Now: "The weeks that generate those claims are usually a timing problem in how the recovery gets staffed, not a people problem, and timing problems move fast."
- Suggested: "The weeks that generate those claims usually trace back to how fast the recovery got staffed, and that part is fixable on a short clock."

(The prospect already got the recovery-runs-a-shift-behind contrast in email 1; this keeps the idea without replaying the move.)

### 3. "Worth" density and the repeated opt-out

Across one airline prospect's journey, "worth" appears roughly seven times (worth a minute, worth 15 minutes, worth me sending, worth a look, worth keeping, worth a fresh look). Swap a few: "does 15 minutes this week or next work?", "should I send it over?", "fancy a look before the season winds down?" (Jack is UK; "fancy" is free realism for him, not for Nathan).

"If you'd rather not hear from me, one word and I'll stop" closes almost every email. Perfect on email 1. By email 3 it is a stamp. Keep it on the first email of each lane, vary or drop after.

### 4. "I'll walk the finance view" - transitive "walk" is sales-speak

Instances: "I'll walk how ops and service teams keep...", "I'll walk the finance view on...", "I'll walk the specific measures where...", "I can walk which of your contracts...". "Walk" without "you through" is an inside-sales dialect prospects notice. Fix globally: "I'll walk you through how...", "happy to show you which contracts...".

### 6. Mid-sequence emails contain no question at all

"six years", "eight weeks", "the cliff is being priced in now", and "the window is more than half spent" all close on the statement "Fifteen minutes and I'll walk..." then the opt-out line. Confident, but a zero-question email gives the reader nothing to answer. Keep the statement close in one lane and A/B a question close ("Have 15 minutes in the next couple weeks?") in the other; let the data decide.
