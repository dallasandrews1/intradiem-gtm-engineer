### The forcing function

The July 22 reorganisation is the headline, and it is not the forcing function. The forcing function was disclosed eleven weeks earlier, in a filing, with a number attached.

In its Q1 2026 MD&A on May 8, 2026, TELUS revised its own full-year restructuring assumption upward and stated the reason, verbatim:

> "Our restructuring and other costs assumption has been revised to approximately $600 million, from approximately $500 million."

> "We estimate total cash restructuring and other disbursements of approximately $550 million, from approximately $450 million."

> "The increase is a result of expanded operational effectiveness programs to support EBITDA and cash flow growth."

That is a named program, a hundred-million-dollar upward revision, a stated rationale and a fiscal year. It is the TELUS equivalent of BCE's $1.5 billion and BMO's $250 million, and unlike either of those it is a revision rather than an announcement, which means the programs were already running and turned out to need more room.

The run rate confirms it. Q1 2026 restructuring and other costs were **$315 million against $97 million in Q1 2025**, a 3.2x increase, which TELUS attributes to:

> "cost efficiency and effectiveness programs, in addition to costs associated with the privatization of TELUS Digital"

### The tension the whole sequence hangs on

TELUS has committed publicly to a position on how it gets there, and that position closes off the obvious lever.

TELUS's official statement on AI in its own contact centres, verbatim:

> "We believe AI, when used responsibly, can augment — not replace — human capabilities and help deliver better outcomes for our customers and communities."

And the July 22 reorganisation release asks for two things in a single sentence, verbatim:

> "This reorganization is designed to drive enhanced customer service, align capital and resources to the highest margin products, services and customer segments, and realize operational efficiencies across the organization."

Read those together and the shape of the problem is specific. TELUS needs $600 million of operational effectiveness. It has said the customer service experience has to get *better*, not just cheaper, in the same sentence. And it has put on the record that its answer to automation is augmentation rather than replacement. Capacity recovered from inside the existing workforce is the one lever that serves all three of those at once: it does not require a reduction, it does not trade against service, and it is the literal definition of augmenting the people already there.

This is the entire opening, and it is TELUS's own language on both sides of it. **The copy leads with the customer-service half of that sentence, never the efficiency half.** See the guardrails section for why that is not a stylistic preference.

### The structural objection, which is also the best thing about this account

TELUS Digital, formerly TELUS International, wholly owned by TELUS Corporation since the privatization closed October 31, 2025, sells contact-centre AI and CX outsourcing commercially to third-party enterprises. It is a Genesys CCaaS and Workforce Engagement Management implementation partner, it announced a Cresta partnership on June 15, 2026, and it markets its own AI platform, Fuel iX, including Agent Assist and Agent Trainer.

So "we do this ourselves" is structural here rather than incidental, and it is the reason the entity-resolution work behind the committee was not optional.

The answer is not to route around it. It is to lead into it, using TELUS Digital's own published research.

TELUS Digital, with Ryan Strategic Advisory, published *Enterprise CX AI: 2026 Global Survey* on June 3, 2026: 815 enterprise CX decision-makers, 12 countries, 19 verticals, fielded Q1 2026. Its finding, verbatim:

> "only 32% of enterprises selected AI-powered quality assurance (QA) and agent coaching"

and

> "two-thirds lack the AI-powered QA and coaching tools needed to assess AI-assisted interactions at scale"

Their own research group put the number at 32%, and **46% say they plan to invest**, a fourteen-point gap between intent and deployment. The opener writes itself: their own arm published the gap, and the interesting question is which side of it TELUS Corporation is on.

That does three things at once. It proves the research was done. It makes the "we do this ourselves" objection the subject of the conversation rather than the end of it. And it is unarguable, because they published it.

**The distinction to hold underneath, and the line the copy must not cross.** TELUS Digital sells CX services to other companies; that is a revenue line, and its research is about *other enterprises*. It says nothing about how TELUS Corporation runs its own operation. The copy asks which side of the number TELUS is on. It never asserts that TELUS is one of the 68%. That inference is unsourced and a TELUS executive will spot it instantly.

### Why the launch is held to September 8

Two reasons, and neither is David Fuller's start date on its own.

The reorganisation was announced on July 22. The directors and vice-presidents beneath the new structure do not yet know their own remit, who they report to, or what survives. That is the worst moment in a company's life to ask someone for twenty minutes about operational change: they have no authority to act and every incentive to defer. Six weeks fixes that.

And Fuller's first week is triage. Week two is when a new group president starts asking what is already in motion, and the objective is to already be a thread someone can point at.

**There is no dark period inside this sequence, and that is a deliberate departure from Bell and BMO.** Both of those went dark around an earnings call that fell inside the send window. TELUS's Q2 call is expected in early August, five weeks *before* Day 1, and its Q3 call will fall in early November, well after Day 9. Manufacturing a blackout here would be cargo-culting the last two documents. What replaces it is a pre-send gate: confirm the Q2 date, and if it has for any reason slipped into September, hold every touch within two business days either side of it.

### The single-entity problem

Every seat in this committee sits in **TELUS Corporation**. None sits in TELUS Digital, TELUS Health or TELUS Agriculture & Consumer Goods, and that was verified per contact rather than assumed from the company object.

This matters more than a hygiene note. TELUS Digital is a wholly owned subsidiary that sells contact-centre AI, CX outsourcing and workforce-engagement implementation to the same enterprises Intradiem sells to. Selling into TELUS Corporation means selling into that subsidiary's parent. Every seat in this document will eventually ask some version of "why would we buy this when we own a company that sells it," and the answer has to be the same answer from all eight of them, or the thread dies the first time two of them compare notes.

The answer, held consistently across the committee: TELUS Digital sells CX services to other companies, and that is a revenue line. It says nothing about the tooling inside TELUS Corporation's own 111,500-person operation, and TELUS Digital's own research is the evidence that the specific layer in question, real-time quality assurance and agent coaching, is the least-deployed part of the market. The messaging is threaded so that the finance seats, the operations seats and the technical seats are visibly making the same argument from different angles, which is the point.

Good, and we do not replace it. Workforce management is a planning system: forecast, schedule, adherence, judged on how good the plan was.
