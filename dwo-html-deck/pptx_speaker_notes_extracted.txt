Here is what makes this problem harder than most organizations realize: it does not live only in the contact center. Most enterprises run multiple structured workforces -- agents taking calls, processors handling claims and disputes, fraud teams reviewing transactions, fulfillment teams managing orders. Each managed independently. Each with its own systems, its own blind spots, its own reactive patterns.
There is no unified view. No coordinated response. And no way to shift resources when one team has capacity and another is overwhelmed.
And the consequences do not stay internal. Every overloaded employee sending the wrong signal to a customer, every missed SLA, every degraded interaction -- those moments compound into something larger: brand damage. In a world where customers have more choices and less patience, your workforce's performance is your brand's performance. You cannot separate them.
NOTE: A "structured workforce" is our term and may need to be defined.
Structured workforce: shift-based teams, work arrives in a queue, not centrally managed, teams are siloed.
=== notesSlide5 ===
Let's talk about what is actually happening inside your structured workforces right now.
Research across the industry consistently shows that only 45 to 65 percent of workforce time is spent doing the work you hired people to do. The rest is lost -- to idle time between interactions, to schedule inefficiencies, to unplanned absences and last-minute scrambles.
Layer attrition on top of that. Thirty to 45 percent of customer-facing workers leave every year on average. In financial services and healthcare it can exceed 60 percent. Each departure costs roughly one to two times that person's annual salary in recruiting, onboarding, and lost productivity. It compounds every single quarter.
And your workforce management team? They are spending 70 percent of their time reacting -- adjusting schedules after the service level has already slipped, firefighting instead of optimizing. The problem is not effort. It is structure. The tools in place were built for a different era.
