Rules I never break

  • Verified claims only. If it is not in the Value Repository with a source, it ships as [UNVERIFIED] or not at all. Regulated buyers, legal exposure, one bad stat kills the whole engine's credibility.
  • Inherit, never replace. The squad is 80% through their Clay build. My job is to make their build great and make them faster, visibly. Any "my system vs. theirs" framing starts the credit war I planned against.
  • Co-shape, never defend. The Q3 numbers are Naveen's rough sketch. I bring strong thinking to shape specifics with him, not a rigid plan executed at him.
  • Measurement before volume. Instrumentation and source tagging exist before scale, so Weeks 5 through 8 are provable, not anecdotal.
  • Dry-run by default. Notifiers and conductor preview-only until explicitly flipped, and preflight prints READY first.
  • Config over code. Thresholds, weights, and copy live in config files. Behavior changes are config edits, reviewable and reversible.
  • Two-mode brand. Intradiem green (Playfair / DM Sans / JetBrains Mono, per gtmstrategy.dallasandrews.dev) for anything Intradiem-context, and this page now runs on it too so the reflex stays consistent. Personal blurple stays for non-Intradiem work only.
  • Reps keep the judgment. AI does the enrichment, people approve the sends, reps own the conversations. I make them faster, never replaceable.
  • WFM players are the layer we sit on, never competitors. Verint, NICE, Calabrio plan the day; DWO orchestrates what happens inside it: idle-time actions, coaching and training delivery, back-office work. When their automation add-ons show up in a deal, wedge against the module and the contact-center-only scope, never the layer itself. Positioning against the layer is a category error that undermines our own integration story.
  • Contact center AND back office, six verticals. Never frame Intradiem as a call-center tool.

Universal screenshare rules

  • A dedicated demo browser profile. Bookmarks only to showable pages. The three eyes-only surfaces, Mission Control, and this page are never open in that profile, ever.
  • One artifact per meeting, maximum. The tour instinct is the enemy; a person who touched one tool that solved their problem tells others. A person who watched ten tools remembers none.
  • Seeded numbers get labeled out loud before anyone asks. "These are placeholder assumptions until the real export lands" said proudly is credibility; discovered silently it is a scandal.
  • Never trigger a live send in a demo. Dry-run views only. The human gate is part of the show, not an obstacle to it.
  • Every meeting ends with a leave-behind link they can open themselves later. Naveen's own standard: nobody remembers slide 14; everyone remembers the calculator they moved with their own hands.
  • Their data beats my demo data. Any artifact that can load the audience's own account does so before the meeting.

The 6-12 month arc

The system is built. The win is political, and it is won by making dependency owners feel like authors of the outcome.

The sequencing logic
Net-new outbound runs on external data I control on day one. Install-base expansion runs on internal data owned by other orgs and gated by governance. So: net-new is the visible wedge, install-base is the parallel access-and-relationship campaign. Never stake early credibility on data I do not yet control.
The arc
0–30: listen, map owners, pilot the wedge with one or two reps. 30–60: one rep books one meeting off a strike plan; claim-approval path secured. 60–90: scorecard to the exec sponsor in their language; install-base signals on cleared data. 4–6 mo: both motions live across the team. 6–12 mo: the brain becomes the company's GTM intelligence layer, owned by me, as the company scales toward exit.
Dependency owners to recruit
Product-usage data: CTO + Busbee/Naveen (allies). Salesforce: Genna + RevOps, read-only first. Slack install: IT, lead with the plugin instead. Procurement (Clay renewal): open the track early. Claims: Marketing + Legal approval workflow. Air cover: CRO John Norton, orient wins to his metric.
Learning agenda (answer in weeks 1–3)
What does the modernized platform expose at event level, and who controls it? Is the CRO's #1 gap net-new or expansion? What is the real claim-approval process? What is already in the stack? Who is my skeptic, who is my champion? How fast does procurement actually move?
